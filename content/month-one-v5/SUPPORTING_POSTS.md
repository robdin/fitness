## BPK02 · November 11 · Before your first run club
1. First run club? Ask before you go.
2. What distance is the actual event?
3. Which pace groups and walk-break options are offered?
4. Who supports the slowest group? Check access and beginner requirements.
5. Use the answers to choose a suitable starting group. Every event is different.

An editorial first-visit checklist. A club example and community questions provide context; neither establishes what your local event offers.

## BPK03 · November 18 · A lunch plan for a full week
1. Tomorrow’s lunch starts with one useful decision.
2. Check the food you already have.
3. Choose one meal that fits your taste, budget and time.
4. List what is missing. Beans, rice and frozen vegetables are one example.
5. One meal is not your whole diet. Adapt the idea to your needs.

A planning example for a busy day, informed by meal-planning guidance. No individual portion target or weight-change promise.

## BPK04 · November 25 · A plan that fits the week you have
1. Dream body. Full calendar. Start with your actual week.
2. Place training in realistic slots and follow your routine’s guidance.
3. Find a usable time and route for a walk. It is one part of activity.
4. Give sleep a place too. Ongoing sleep difficulties deserve professional advice.
5. When life changes the plan, choose your next realistic step.

A planning framework across strength, movement and recovery. These examples do not form a complete individualized program.
