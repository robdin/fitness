## BCK02 · November 11 · Before your first run club
1. First run club? Send a quick message.
2. I'm new. What distance is the event?
3. What pace groups are there? Are walk breaks welcome?
4. Who stays with the last group? Is there anything I need to bring or know about access?
5. Check those answers before choosing your first session. Every event is different.

An editorial first-visit checklist. A club example and community questions provide context; neither establishes what your local event offers.

## BCK03 · November 18 · Tomorrow’s lunch, one decision at a time
1. Tomorrow’s lunch might already be in the kitchen.
2. Pick one meal. Check what you've got.
3. Rice, beans and frozen vegetables could become a bowl you enjoy.
4. List only the missing ingredients. Leave room for preparation and cleanup.
5. One meal idea, not your whole diet. Make it fit your needs and taste.

A planning example for a busy day, informed by meal-planning guidance. No individual portion target or weight-change promise.

## BCK04 · November 25 · A plan that fits the week you have
1. Dream body. Full calendar. Start with your actual week.
2. Check your plan's session length, then add travel and changing.
3. A familiar walk can fit a lunch break. It is one part of activity.
4. Make room for sleep where you can. Ongoing sleep trouble deserves professional advice.
5. Missed a session? Check your plan and choose the next visit you can make.

A planning framework across strength, movement and recovery. These examples do not form a complete individualized program.
