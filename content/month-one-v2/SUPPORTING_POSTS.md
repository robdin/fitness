## K01 · November 4 · Build around your actual week

1. Your real week is the starting point.
2. Mark the gym days you can reliably attend.
3. Choose a structured routine that fits those days.
4. Read its effort targets. More visits and more failure are not automatic requirements.
5. Record what you complete. Review the pattern over several weeks.

**Caption:** A planning checklist for a busy week. It does not promise that every two-day routine matches more training. Save this for your next planning session.

## K03 · November 18 · Before you change your range of motion

1. One study does not rewrite every exercise.
2. Which exercise and people were studied?
3. Which range of motion was actually compared?
4. Which outcome and measurement site changed?
5. No clear difference in a small study does not prove universal equality.

**Caption:** Ask these questions before turning a technique headline into a whole-program change. The partial-range studies cover specific exercises and outcomes.

## K04 · November 25 · A program needs more than a ranking

1. Top-ranked exercises are ideas, not a complete week.
2. Choose a goal and a schedule you can repeat.
3. Know the weekly work and effort the plan asks for.
4. Use a progression rule. Adding reps can be part of it.
5. Keep a comparable log, then review the plan rather than collecting more badges.

**Caption:** A practical program checklist: goal, schedule, training work, progression and a log. Which one needs the most attention in your plan?
