Draft audience resource · 26 October 2026. A planning aid for adult beginners and returners, not a prescribed workout, diet or treatment. Choose one section for the situation you have today; completing every field is not a prerequisite to starting.

## If you are not sure what to do at the gym

Send this question to your gym or an appropriate instructor:

> I'm new. What orientation or beginner-plan help is available? I can attend on [days], with about [time] available including travel. I need a routine for the equipment here and help learning its setup and instructions.

Check what the service includes and whether there is a fee. A suitable starting plan should explain the exercises, sets and repetitions, rest, and how to progress. Ask about anything you cannot follow before trying to improvise it. This checklist helps you request usable instruction; it does not certify a plan or replace equipment-specific guidance.

**Your next action:** send the question or find the gym's actual orientation information. If you already have a suitable routine, put its instructions in your phone and identify one setup question to ask.

## My real week

Copy the four lines that apply into your notes:

```text
MY NEXT SESSION
Day / time / place:
Time needed for the whole trip:
Routine or activity I'm following:
If that slot falls through, my next step:
```

**Worked scheduling example — illustrative, not a recommended routine:**

```text
Day / time / place: Tuesday, 6:15 PM, gym near work
Whole trip: leave 6:00 PM; back around 7:10 PM
Routine: the Tuesday session in my beginner plan
If work runs late: check my plan's rescheduling guidance,
then choose the next workable session in my calendar
```

Count travel, changing and any cleanup that matter in your situation. Check the actual length of your routine rather than squeezing it into the example's time. A fallback may mean adapting with appropriate guidance, rescheduling or resting. A short walk is a different activity, not an equivalent replacement for every missed strength session.

## A walk that fits something you already do

```text
After:
My usable route / place:
If that option is unavailable:
```

Example: “After lunch, I can use the familiar route near my office. If the weather makes it unsuitable, I'll use an accessible indoor option or choose another time.” Choose activity that fits your ability and circumstances. This is a planning example, not the full adult activity target or a guaranteed habit technique.

## Tomorrow's lunch

```text
One meal I want to make:
What I already have:
Only the missing items:
When preparation will fit:
```

Example: “For the rice bowl I've chosen, I already have rice and beans. I'll check the other ingredients and list only what is missing.” Choose food suitable for your needs, budget and preferences. The example does not specify portions or represent a complete diet; follow applicable food handling and storage instructions.

## The smallest useful training log

Use the exercise and instructions from your actual routine. Record enough to understand what happened next time.

| Date / exercise | Setup and load used | Repetitions completed | Useful note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [date / exercise from plan] | [equipment setting; actual load] | [reps for each set] | [one setup or performance note] |

**Invented example of how to write an entry, not a load or rep recommendation:**

| Date / exercise | Setup and load used | Repetitions completed | Useful note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tuesday / seated row in my plan | Machine A; seat 3; display 10 kg | 8, 8, 7 | Asked staff to show the seat adjustment |

Keep units and equipment identity clear; machine displays are not interchangeable measures across different machines. Use the actual effort/rest/progression instructions in your plan. A training entry records performance and context; it does not measure muscle growth or diagnose a plateau.

**Your next action:** make one blank row in your notes before your next visit. You do not need a new app or a ten-metric dashboard to start recording useful information.

## If you missed a session

```text
What got in the way:
My plan's guidance for returning:
Next realistic session:
One practical adjustment:
```

Example: “Work ran late. I'll check my routine's guidance, choose the next workable session, and put my bag by the door beforehand.” Do not automatically double the next workout to punish a missed one. Illness, injury or recovery needs may call for a different decision and appropriate advice; this is a scheduling prompt, not an individual recovery prescription.

## Editorial grounding

The planning structures are original applications informed by the Turuan audit and existing Blueprint content. Named mental models are editorial metaphors, not validated fitness treatments. Relevant boundaries and support:

- [CDC adult activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html): a short walking example is not the complete activity target.
- [Food Standards Scotland meal planning](https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/consumer-advice/healthy-eating/planning-and-shopping/meal-planning): check existing food and plan what is needed.
- [Carrera et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7872013/): simple gym scheduling prompts did not improve attendance in that trial. No adherence improvement is promised by this worksheet.
- The gym request, logging format and restart prompts are editorial aids. They do not prescribe an exercise program or promise results.
