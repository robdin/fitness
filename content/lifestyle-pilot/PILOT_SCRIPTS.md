## Shared production approach

Start with a specific situation. Demonstrate the answer with one completed phone note, calendar entry or message. End with the doable action; keep optional conversation questions in the caption. Use D as the person experiencing the situation only where a suitable scene exists or is deliberately produced. Do not claim the three existing still-companion poses provide new acting footage.

Use the new casual, relatable voice direction: a friendly peer explaining one useful thing. Keep essential claim boundaries, readable captions and source cues. These drafts are not required to fit the old audio length. Measure new narration naturally before setting exact timings. A planning range of approximately 20–35 seconds is an editorial target, not an algorithm rule.

Mental models are optional background reading. The viewer's situation drives the script; no model label, metadata field or framework is required. Drop the model if it adds a detour or another task. The examples below stand on their own.

## LP01 · Before your first gym visit

**Based on:** BP01 orientation.  
**Hook:** “New gym. No idea what to do first?”  
**Viewer outcome:** know what to ask and send an actual orientation request.  
**Claim class:** editorial starting checklist, not a routine prescription.

| Beat | Exact draft narration | Visible scene/object |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | New gym. No idea what to do first? | Gym entrance or D with a bag; the uncertainty is visible |
| Answer | Send the gym a quick message before you go. | Phone message begins filling in |
| Example | Try: I'm new here. Can someone show me how to set up the equipment in my plan? | Exact short message, clearly labeled as an example |
| Boundary | No plan yet? Ask what beginner help they offer and what it costs. | Available days and equipment appear in the longer message; no invented workout |
| Action | Once you've got the instructions, save them in your phone. | Actual orientation details and saved routine note |
| Close | Start with that message. You don't have to guess your way around. | Hold the completed message long enough to read |

**Caption:** Before your first visit, check what orientation or beginner-plan help the gym actually offers and whether there is a fee. The message is a starting question, not a promise that every gym supplies programming. Bring the routine's instructions and ask for setup help where needed.

**Comprehension:** viewer can name the message they would send and understands that a routine and setup instruction still need to come from an appropriate source. A critical error is assuming every gym provides individualized programming for free or that this clip is itself a workout.

## LP08 · Count the whole trip

**Based on:** BP08 realistic scheduling.  
**Hook:** “Got half an hour for the gym?”  
**Viewer outcome:** budget realistic door-to-door time before choosing a session slot.  
**Claim class:** editorial time accounting and planning. No equivalent-gains or adherence claim.

| Beat | Exact draft narration | Visible scene/object |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Got half an hour for the gym? | One hypothetical free calendar slot; not a recommended session length |
| Answer | Is that time to train, or time until you need to be home? | A workout block expands to reveal travel and changing |
| Example | Getting there, changing, and getting back all count. | Calendar with work and social plans already present |
| Boundary | Check how long the workout in your plan actually takes. | Routine duration sits inside the larger door-to-door block |
| Action | Then put the whole trip in your calendar. | A completed illustrative appointment with departure/return |
| Close | Does it fit? Great. If it doesn't, look for a slot that does. | Calendar action remains the final frame |

**Caption:** Use the actual length and instructions of your routine, plus the practical time around it. Leave room for the week you have. This is a planning tool; putting a session in a calendar does not guarantee attendance or results.

**Comprehension:** viewer includes relevant travel/changing time and chooses an available slot, without interpreting the example as a prescribed routine or guaranteed equal outcome. Do not attach a CDC badge to the editorial door-to-door arithmetic; cite any retained scientific teaching separately.

## LP16 · Choose the next return

**Based on:** BP16 missed workout.  
**Hook:** “Work ran late and you missed the gym?”  
**Viewer outcome:** make a specific return decision instead of escalating punishment or abandoning the week.  
**Claim class:** editorial scheduling, not an individual recovery recommendation.

| Beat | Exact draft narration | Visible scene/object |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Work ran late and you missed the gym? | Missed appointment; no red failure score |
| Answer | Let's figure out when you can go next. | Routine's schedule beside calendar |
| Example | Check your plan's missed-day advice, then find a slot you can actually make. | One barrier becomes one practical calendar adjustment |
| Boundary | You don't automatically owe yourself a double workout. | Normal session entry, no extra punishment block |
| Action | Put the next visit in your phone. Pack your bag if that helps. | Completed date/place note and bag preparation |
| Close | Start with that next visit. | Hold the actual next-session entry |

**Caption:** This is a practical response to a scheduling interruption. Illness, injury or recovery needs may require a different decision. Choose the next step that fits your plan and circumstances; a reset prompt does not guarantee a habit.

**Comprehension:** viewer names the next feasible session and one practical barrier adjustment, preserving the routine's guidance. Critical errors include automatically doubling the workout, ignoring recovery needs or treating a different brief activity as equivalent to the missed training.
