# Body of our dreams · dreamy but tactical

Prepared 23 October 2026; updated after the owner selected **Blueprint** and requested production. The [Blueprint style guide](../blueprint/STYLE_GUIDE.md) and [V5 month](../../docs/MONTH_ONE_V5.md) are now active. The founding story supersedes earlier name recommendations while preserving scientific standards, useful priorities and lifestyle fit. The public brand name remains undecided. Daylight and Afterglow below preserve the earlier comparison.

## The idea

**Your dream body. Your real life.**

“Dream body” is personal: how you want to look, feel and move, and the life you want your body to support. Aesthetic ambition belongs here alongside health, capability and enjoyment. The brand helps people work toward their goals without suggesting that one physique is everyone's destination or that every desired outcome is achievable on a fixed timeline.

**Positioning:** science-backed fitness for people with meaningful body goals and full lives. We turn confusing advice into useful priorities and workable routines across strength, cardio, everyday movement, nutrition and recovery.

**Mission:** Help people work toward the body, health and confidence they want through science-backed training, movement and nutrition that fit everyday life.

**Vision:** A world where caring for your body is woven into how you live.

**Public descriptor:** Science-backed fitness for a full life.

**Supporting line:** Big dream. Real-life plan.

“What works. What matters. What fits.” remains an internal editorial check. Science is how we evaluate advice; 80/20 is how we prioritize; lifestyle fit is how we make a next step usable. Do not promise 80% of results from 20% of effort. The ratio is not a universal fitness finding.

## Founder story and voice

Founder-story draft, based only on the owner's account:

> This started with a friend saying she wanted the “body of her dreams” while living a busy life. The phrase became a running joke between us—and a goal we meant. We wanted to look how we wanted, feel good and take care of our health while still making room for everything else in our lives. This brand grew from that question: how do we make fitness part of the life we're already living?

The emotional tone is sincere aspiration with a knowing smile. Be warm about goals and precise about the work. Humor comes from real situations: the full calendar, the improvised lunch, the workout plan with more free time than its owner. Let the joke land without mocking someone's body, motivation, budget or starting point.

| Job | Example copy |
|---|---|
| Campaign | “Dream body. Full calendar.” |
| Practical invitation | “Start with the week you have.” |
| Progress conversation | “What do you want to look, feel or move differently?” |
| Community question | “Where does your routine stop fitting your life?” |
| Recovery from disruption | “Missed Tuesday. What's the next realistic session?” |
| Evidence language | “Here's what this study supports—and where the answer is less certain.” |

Use one specific question per episode. Ask about goals and constraints without requesting public body measurements or health histories. Avoid “effortless,” “your dream body in 30 days,” universal optimal routines, fake before/after transformations and claims that the brand itself is scientifically proven.

**Draft bio:** Your dream body. Your real life. Science-backed training, movement, food & recovery. Useful priorities. Sources included.

## What the existing research contributes

Initial research remains useful for particular beginner questions. Future scripts must preserve adult/youth scope; a broad brand promise does not make adult prescriptions suitable for teenagers.

## Visual system: aspiration with a usable plan

**Owner selection: Blueprint.** Daylight was the earlier recommendation; the owner chose Blueprint. These names label treatments, not public brand names. [Concept tokens](tokens.json) retain all three palettes; [production tokens](../blueprint/tokens.json) define the active implementation.

| Treatment | Look and composition | Best reason to choose it | Question to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daylight · historical alternative** | Warm paper, cloud blue and lilac light; a generous editorial headline above a crisp planning card; cobalt actions | Feels optimistic and spacious while remaining useful | Does the fitness descriptor make the category obvious? |
| **Blueprint · selected** | Paper and cobalt; a compact grid, numbered decisions and a smaller soft halo; strong alignment | Makes the practical method immediately visible | Does it feel inviting or like productivity homework? |
| **Afterglow** | Deep ink, a lilac/peach horizon and warm paper information panels; a large central aspiration | Gives the dream a stronger cinematic presence | Does it drift toward sleep, beauty or vague wellness? |

**Type:** keep Space Grotesk for explanations, numbers, captions and source cues. Add Fraunces italic selectively for an aspirational word or short campaign line. Keep scientific qualifications in the same readable sans serif as the answer. Font licensing/distribution must be checked when bundling assets for production.

**Color:** use light and gradients as atmosphere. Put essential copy on solid, tested surfaces. Preserve cobalt from Training Club; the new warm paper and ink are close relatives of the existing palette. Lilac and cloud blue add the dream. Lime is optional, reserved for a small useful action cue rather than a full-screen default. The palette is a creative choice; no research here proves that these colors increase trust.

**Graphic language:** horizons, open space and soft light around crisp calendars, routine cards, grocery lists, routes and exercise diagrams. The recurring device is an aspiration paired with a usable next step. Do not draw invented health scores or render illustrative schedules as study data. Label an editorial checklist as a checklist; reserve citations for claims they support.

**Motion/audio:** retain the approved narration. Use a decisive text or object change when the explanation advances; a soft transition can supply atmosphere. Keep speech intelligible and captions static long enough to read. A dreamy identity does not require slow answer delivery, a logo introduction, whispering or a new soundtrack for every post.

**People:** no AI character or idealized physique as the recurring presenter. Existing typography/diagram production can carry the identity. Any later real-life photography needs appropriate rights and honest context; do not add it to every episode merely to fill space.

**Vertical production constraints:** preserve 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, critical text within x=75–925/y=240–1500, captions at least the current 43 px, at most two lines and the existing 20-character-per-second ceiling. These are house constraints pending actual app previews. Expressive type is for brief covers, never dense instructions. Check font loading, line breaks and source cue readability at phone size after implementation.

## Content application

The brand must visibly cover more than lifting. Keep the [proposed five-topic expansion](../../research/brand-culture-2026-10/MONTH_ONE_APPLICATION.md): walking, first run-club visit, practical groceries, sleep routines and recreational activity. Those five topics are researched and produced as BP04, BP07, BP11, BP14 and BP15 in the [V5 month](../../docs/MONTH_ONE_V5.md). Their [research update](../../research/blueprint-v5/README.md) separates community demand from primary guidance. Producing them does not establish demand for the broader promise.

Three reusable series are sufficient for the pilot:

- **Dream / Plan:** turn a goal into a question about someone's actual week. Format: recognizable ambition → one constraint → planning example → next step.
- **Worth your effort:** explain which decision deserves attention first, with sources and boundaries. B09's protein-powder question fits here.
- **Life happens:** show how to respond when a routine meets interruptions. B16 is a strong existing example.

Examples below are new packaging proposals, not approved replacement scripts:

| Slot | Proposed opening / graphic | What must remain |
|---|---|---|
| B08, realistic gym schedule | “Dream body. Full calendar. Start with the days you can actually use.” A simple available-time example turns into a planning card | Existing routine guidance and limits; do not imply a universal two-day result |
| B09, protein powder | “What belongs on your starting list?” A useful-priority card | Existing explanation of optional convenience and dietary/age scope |
| B16, missed session | “Your plan met your week. Now what?” Move an illustrative session to the next realistic slot | Non-punitive return guidance; no catch-up challenge |
| Proposed P-MOVE-01 | “Where could a walk fit into a day you already have?” Link an illustrative walk to an existing part of the day | New evidence review; no invented step target or habit guarantee |
| Proposed P-FOOD-01 | “What would make tomorrow's lunch easier?” A usable grocery/list decision | New nutrition/access review; no transformation promise |

The selected style now covers all BP01–BP16 episodes, including BP08 and BP16. The new BP03 source-label pair is isolated under CV2 and checked for matched audio, timing and visual differences. Original B03/CV1 files remain historical. BP04 supplies a separate route-motion control.

Do not front-load every post with “dream body.” Let the identity appear through the subject, constraint and useful answer. Keep the current four-video cadence and 12-hour weekly hands-on ceiling. Reuse templates; log first-time design work separately and include it in the overall launch cost. Measure recurring labor instead of assuming the new look is cheaper.

## Naming direction

The origin phrase can anchor the story even if the public name differs. **Dreams in Practice** is the lead creative territory to explore now: it joins aspiration with repeated action and allows strength, movement, food and recovery. It needs the fitness descriptor and testing for sleep, journaling or self-help associations.

Two alternatives to explore are **Built into Life** (strongest integration cue; less dreamy) and **Everyday Becoming** (warm aspiration; less immediate category clarity). These are creative candidates, not cleared or selected names. Earlier Essential Effort and Reason & Routine remain historical options; neither is still the default recommendation under this new emotional brief.

A limited public-web screen on 23 October found existing “Dreambody” uses in a [fitness/nutrition app](https://www.dreambody.fit/support) and an [AI body-photo editor](https://www.dreambody.ai/). That is a practical reason to seek distinction, not a legal conclusion. Limited exact-name search results for Dreams in Practice did not establish availability. The two additional candidates have not been screened. Check exact/near names, relevant marks, domains and platform handles for a chosen shortlist before adoption; no registrations or account changes have occurred.

## Validation after owner selection

Use the revised [test plan](../../research/brand-culture-2026-10/TEST_PLAN.md). First test meaning: does “dream body” feel self-defined, does the account clearly cover fitness, and can people explain the useful promise? Blueprint is selected, so the optional three-treatment comparison is deferred; first check the selected design's readability and comprehension. Ask for the takeaway and next action before preference. Separately test name comprehension in neutral type once the shortlist is agreed.

Include adults across genders and busy life contexts because the proposed vision is broader than the original research sample. Eighteen exploratory participants remain a practical target, not enough to establish demographic effects. No audience test has been conducted. Do not claim a validated winner from this design review.

The historical draft checks below are separate from production verification. The V5 package checks scripts, source cues, text bounds, captions, media decoding and matched controls; full listening and target-app previews remain pending. Keep the original go/no-go criteria: repeated audience interest, understandable content and a process that fits 12 hands-on hours per week. Retention alone cannot establish that the aspirational framing helps people.
