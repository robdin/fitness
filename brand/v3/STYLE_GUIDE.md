# Rep & Reason · production style guide V3

Prepared 22 October 2026. The project owner selected **Training Club** on 22 October 2026. Training Lab and Coach’s Notebook remain selectable visual packages. No audience preference test has been completed. The brand name remains provisional and is not trademark-cleared.

## Positioning and language

Help a new lifter make the next gym visit less confusing. Speak with confidence about what the evidence supports and plainly about what is uncertain. The audience is male beginners aged 16–35, with different circumstances inside that range. Do not assume a particular body, sexuality, income, ability or goal. Under-18 instruction needs explicit age-appropriate context.

**Voice:** direct, encouraging, specific. “Bring a routine and ask about setup.” “That is a performance comparison, not a muscle measurement.” “Which term should we explain next?” Avoid “alpha,” “weak,” “no excuses,” “secret hack,” “scientifically guaranteed,” and fake urgency. Humor may come from a recognizable gym situation; it should not target a beginner’s body or competence.

**Promise:** one usable decision. **Proof:** a visible explanation and an accessible source note. **Sign-off:** Know why. Train better. **Name:** Rep & Reason, with the ampersand; do not abbreviate the name everywhere before recognition exists.

## System A · Training Lab

- Background `#0A1930`; light text `#F4F7EF`; lime `#C5F257`; dark accent text `#102338`; panel `#203650`; secondary text `#C3CFDF`.
- Fine grid, numbered modules, small square corners and an R² experiment-style mark paired with the full name.
- Space Grotesk, medium/semibold treatment; measured object motion and restrained transitions.
- Best use: comparisons, logs and decoding a training claim. Risk: looking more scientific than the evidence. Label examples and avoid decorative statistics.

## System B · Training Club — selected direction

- Warm background `#F5F3EA`; ink `#14221C`; lime `#CAEF51`; cobalt `#2149CA`; secondary text `#415248`.
- Strong rectangular fields, a vertical rule, an R/R corner mark, oversized practical objects and a clear active state.
- Space Grotesk with strong headline hierarchy; short uppercase display lines, ordinary sentence-case captions.
- Lime indicates the current decision or useful detail; cobalt is a framing element, not a warning code. Text on lime uses ink. Never use light text on lime.
- Best use: a broad beginner series with energy and a repeatable template. Risk: generic sports styling; the consistent mark, explanation sequence and voice need recognition testing.

## System C · Coach’s Notebook

- Paper `#F6EDDE`; ink `#302A27`; brick `#973F2D`; light accent text `#FFF8ED`; card `#FFF9F0`; secondary text `#67584D`.
- Margin rules, rounded note cards, simple check marks and an R mark paired with the name.
- Space Grotesk at lighter display weight; calmer framing, fewer strong color fields. It remains readable, not faint stationery.
- Best use: planning and reflective checklists. Risk: a worksheet feel; use specific objects and changes rather than paragraphs.

The three systems share a licensed type family to keep deployment reproducible; hierarchy, weight, framing and shape differ. These are package choices, not isolated tests of a single color.

## Composition and type

The master is 1080 × 1920. Keep critical text approximately within x=76–925 and y=250–1490; the right edge and lower region are deliberately quiet for overlays. These are conservative house margins, not guaranteed universal platform safe areas. Inspect the real uploader before release.

Brand row: y≈250, 23–28 px metadata. Headline: y≈354, up to two lines around 88 px, fitted only as needed. Explanatory object: y≈630–1140, with main labels generally at least 30–40 px. Scope line: y≈1170, secondary 23 px; material limits also appear in spoken content and the main headline where relevant. Captions: y≈1242, normally 43 px, at most two lines. Sign-off: y≈1460. Do not shrink a central claim into a disclaimer.

At a 360 px-wide display, 43 px master captions are about 14 px. The automated bound check is only a first pass: inspect on a phone for glare, speed, platform controls and actual legibility. Text/background pairs use a minimum 4.5:1 house benchmark; geometry and non-text highlights are tested for visibility separately. Do not communicate a decision through color alone: labels, position and ticks also carry it.

## Illustrations, data and movement

Draw original equipment icons, calendars, logs, grouped repetitions and food categories. These are explanatory symbols, not instructions for exact exercise technique. Do not animate a body performing a lift unless qualified form review is available. Never invent a study chart; an illustrative log is explicitly labeled. No ideal physique is used as a promise of belonging.

Motion should reveal a relationship: repetitions group into sets, a log gains a rep, a rest interval advances, or a choice becomes active. Use brief eased motion, stable reading positions and hard cuts when the meaning changes. No flashing, artificial urgency, bouncing whole paragraphs or unrelated stock footage. A static comparison holds interpolated movement still while preserving scene changes and the same audio.

The first useful answer should begin early, with about five seconds as an editorial target to inspect from real audio. Do not speed up the approved voice to force a duration. If a script is long, remove a secondary idea. Preserve useful pauses and captions after the final sentence.

## Audio and editorial identity

Use the approved Ron Narration v2 voice at natural pitch and speed. Normalize the narration master consistently and check true peaks, decode and transcript. No music is required for the pilot; adding music later is a separate licensed production choice, not an assumed growth lever. An AI-generated voice is not a claim that a credentialed coach reviewed the advice. DEX, if introduced later, is a fictional guide and optional brand element.

## Covers and supporting posts

A cover names the same problem the video answers. Keep the key object and short title central enough to inspect in the platform’s crop. Do not promise a transformation, secret or result absent from the video. Carousels use one thought per panel and a visible page number. Stories have a prompt and an explanatory follow-up; the prompt reserves a blank region for a real native poll. The response is never locked behind engagement.

Descriptions and captions provide source links, scope and a practical action. Sources are reviewed for the specific claim; a list of papers does not create blanket authority. Separate an illustrative checklist from a research result.

## Recognition and fit test

Use the exact B01 comparisons, with identical narration and timing. Assign the first version in balanced random blocks; do not show all three first and then call the first answer unaided. Ask: main point, next action, limitation, readability, trust and relevance. Only then show alternatives and ask preference. Record remembered name/mark and misattribution. Preference is descriptive; comprehension failures take priority. A project-owner selection is a direction decision, not target-market validation.

The initial adult screen does not establish suitability for 16–17-year-olds. Retain youth scope and qualified supervision context in the content, and describe that validation gap honestly. No research participants have been recruited or contacted.
