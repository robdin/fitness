## 4. Positioning and ideal audience

### The current brand brief

**Positioning:** science-backed fitness for people with meaningful body goals and full lives. Turn confusing advice into useful priorities and workable routines across strength, cardio, everyday movement, nutrition and recovery.

**Mission:** help people work toward the body, health and confidence they want through science-backed training, movement and nutrition that fit everyday life.

**Vision:** caring for the body becomes woven into how people live, rather than a separate box to check.

**Promise:** “Your dream body. Your real life.”

**Public descriptor:** “Science-backed fitness for a full life.”

**Editorial test:** “What works. What matters. What fits.”

The 80/20 idea means prioritization. It does not establish a universal promise that 20% of effort yields 80% of fitness results. “Dream body” includes self-defined appearance, health, confidence and capability; it must not prescribe one physique or guarantee a transformation timeline.

### Working ideal follower profile

At this stage the ICP is an ideal follower hypothesis, not a proven paying-customer profile.

| Dimension | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| Core person | Busy fitness beginner or returner who already wants to get fit and needs a workable path |
| Initial adult audience | Roughly 18–35, across genders, with work, study, relationship or care commitments |
| Situation | Starting, restarting, or struggling to sustain occasional effort |
| Desired progress | Feel confident in appearance, healthier, more energetic and physically capable |
| Friction | Conflicting advice, decision fatigue, impractical schedules and repeated restarts |
| Reason to follow | The next decision becomes clearer and fits an ordinary week |
| Useful audience language | “I want the body of my dreams, but I also want a life outside fitness.” |

Earlier work focused on male lifters aged 16–35. The founder story broadened the intended audience. The adult inclusive audience is a positioning choice requiring new validation; earlier male-focused and adult-study findings do not automatically generalize across gender, age or circumstance. Keep under-18 content and adult evidence scope distinct.

D's male appearance and anime treatment are creative choices. Anime fandom is not a validated requirement of the ICP. The character should embody recognizable routines and friction rather than determine who is welcome.

### Discovery hypotheses

People may encounter a problem through friends, Shorts/Reels or a gym visit; investigate it through YouTube search, Google, community forums, a gym instructor or an AI assistant; then look for a practical routine, clearer demonstration or setup help. These pathways were inferred from public material, not measured clickstream data.

For another idea, replace the audience and job before selecting its mascot, tone or platform. Interview about a recent concrete incident: what happened, what the person tried, what it cost, where they looked and what remained unresolved. Do not begin by asking whether they like your proposed brand.

## 5. The reusable operating framework

The repeatable loop is:

**Audience problem → demand evidence → supported answer → original script → visible explanation → verified media → viewer understanding → real distribution → fixed-window measurement → bounded decision.**

| Stage | Required input | Required output | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Charter | Owner intent, constraints, domain | One-page idea brief, success criteria, time/cost ceiling, authority boundaries | Unknowns are explicit; success is more specific than “go viral” |
| 1. Audience discovery | Recent problems and alternatives | ICP hypothesis, journey, demand log, question bank | Several concrete problems have traceable origins |
| 2. Evidence review | Candidate questions | Source register, claim map, limits, editorial-versus-factual distinction | Every material factual claim has adequate support or is narrowed/deferred |
| 3. Editorial selection | Demand, evidence, audience stage | Ranked backlog and learning sequence | First topics serve the intended starting point |
| 4. Brand | Promise, category, audience | Name candidates, descriptor, voice, tokens, reusable components | Category and useful promise are understandable; preference is labeled as preference |
| 5. Representative pilot | Three different topic types | Three reviewable clips with fixed scripts and cost/time records | Readability, meaning and production feasibility are inspected before a full batch |
| 6. Calendar and production | Pilot treatment and claim-reviewed scripts | Core posts, supporting material, captions, source-linked posting copy | Assets are complete, identifiable and technically verified |
| 7. Viewer screen | Frozen exact exports and instrument | Unaided takeaway/action/limit responses, failure records | Recurring consequential misunderstanding is repaired |
| 8. Distribution | Reviewed assets, account identity, launch decision | Actual scheduled/published IDs and timestamps | The provider confirms the intended account, media and state |
| 9. Measurement | Real platform exports and labor logs | Separate platform readouts, usefulness coding, production costs | Observation windows and denominators are comparable and missingness is visible |
| 10. Decision | Frozen rules and complete evidence | Go, iterate, no-go or insufficient evidence, with next horizon | The conclusion follows the rules rather than the most flattering metric |

This refined sequence is the recommended reuse method. The fitness project performed several full creative revisions before audience tests; do not copy that sequence as the efficient default for a new idea.

Use stable identities throughout: source ID → claim ID → episode ID → script version/hash → audio hash → media hash → experiment assignment or publication ID → observation window → decision. A filename alone is not sufficient provenance.

## 6. Research method and findings

### Keep four kinds of evidence separate

| Evidence type | What it can support | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary research / official guidance | The bounded factual answer and its scope | Demand for this account or guaranteed audience behavior |
| Community questions | Language, uncertainty, barriers and candidate topics | Scientific correctness, representative prevalence or demographics |
| Creator material | Packaging examples, explanation patterns, product/category context | Why a post succeeded, private retention, profit or a replicable causal formula |
| Actual project observations | This audience's responses to an identified asset under recorded conditions | General effects beyond the design, sample and observation period |

Desk research is completed work. Audience experiments are prepared work. Neither should be described as the other.

### The five research perspectives used

**Psychology:** make uncertainty recognizable, provide an achievable next step and preserve choice. Curiosity studies, motivation studies and conversation research suggested hypotheses about competence, relevance and responsiveness. They did not validate a Shorts growth formula. Questions about a process or constraint are more useful editorial inputs than requests for sensitive body details. Attention, comprehension and practical usefulness need separate measures.

**Short-form video and distribution:** put the actual problem on the first frame, answer early and animate a relationship that helps explain the answer. No logo introduction. Six writing beats do not require six identical cards. The research did not establish one compulsory posting cadence, universal best hour, magic duration or algorithmic weight for shares. Platform guidance and metric definitions need rechecking before a new launch.

**Beginner journey:** sequence orientation, terminology and setup before advanced optimization. Public questions covered first visits, conflicting routines, sets, starting weights, soreness, supplements and interruptions. This caused the N-series to be rewritten into the B-series. Forum demographics were largely unknown; public examples were paraphrased without usernames or private details.

**Brand:** clarity, category recognition and an inviting promise come before aesthetic preference. The project compared Training Lab, Training Club and Coach's Notebook, then later Daylight, Blueprint and Afterglow. The owner selected Training Club first, Blueprint later. Neither selection resulted from an audience test.

**Writing:** use specific tension with an honest payoff. Draft the useful answer first, identify a recognizable situation, then write the hook. Keep one main idea, one visible explanation, the material boundary and one next step. If the accurate answer needs too many caveats for the format, narrow the question rather than rushing.

### Citation and character findings

The follow-up trust review examined seven study records plus a correction. It found mixed, context-dependent evidence. Citations can influence perceived truth without making a statement correct; a short-video study did not find a credibility benefit from its scientific-reference treatment. Therefore sources remain part of accountability, while their visual presentation is separately testable.

Aspiration, attractiveness, muscularity, similarity, attainability and competence are different constructs. The reviewed work did not establish that a more muscular character earns more follows from this audience. Some work suggested upward-comparison motivation; other work raised relatability or body-image concerns. The earlier muscularity experiment was retired before participants. D is a new creative direction, not the winner of that retired study.

### Culture and trend research

The project reviewed public examples from community discussions, creator packaging, platform/trend reports, memes and naming territory. Native access and visual coverage were uneven. The work does not amount to representative sampling of Reddit, X, TikTok, Instagram or YouTube feeds.

A reusable listening cycle can allocate approximately one hour per week:

- 20 minutes: recurring questions in two or three relevant communities.
- 15 minutes: native short-video discovery where access exists.
- 10 minutes: relevant X searches or lists when accessible.
- 10 minutes: meme chronology, original context and counterexamples.
- 5 minutes: choose one testable cultural opening or choose no new meme.

Record original URL, publication/event date, observation date, platform, actual accessible material, relevance, independent origins, counterexamples, plain-language fallback and production burden. Two independently originating examples are an editorial screen for a possible cross-community opportunity, not a statistical definition of a trend. An excellent evergreen question needs no trend claim.

### Research record template

For each source: `id`, `kind`, `title`, `url`, `author/organization`, `published_at`, `checked_at`, `access_level`, `population/context`, `finding_or_question`, `limitations`, `supported_claims`, `episode_ids`, `corrections`, `rights_or_reuse_notes`.

Keep full text, abstract-only access, indexed text, metadata and actual watched video distinct. Log unavailable material. Check corrections/retractions where relevant. Do not imply that a creator's video was watched frame by frame if only its title and description were available.

## 7. From demand to a content backlog

### Admission questions

For a candidate topic, answer:

1. Which specific person experiences which specific problem?
2. What recent evidence shows that the question exists?
3. What decision can a short explanation help them make?
4. Which factual claims need support and what are their limits?
5. What object, comparison or scene will explain the answer visually?
6. Can we produce and review it within capacity?
7. What could a viewer wrongly infer, and how will we detect that?

A useful reusable prioritization rubric is high/medium/low for audience relevance, strength of support, visible explanatory potential, practical usefulness and production effort. This is a proposed editorial tool, not a ranking model that has already predicted results. Preserve the reasons; do not invent a “virality score.”

### Question patterns

| Pattern | Fitness example | General adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decode a term | “What does three by eight mean?” | Explain the instruction a beginner sees but cannot use |
| Resolve a choice | “Two gym days—where do you start?” | Show criteria under a real constraint |
| Correct a misleading signal | “Not sore—did the workout count?” | Replace a false proxy with a better observation |
| Invite prediction | “Same weight, one more rep: progress?” | Let the viewer inspect a bounded example |
| Remove unnecessary complexity | “Do I need protein powder to start?” | Clarify what is optional and what matters first |
| Recover from interruption | “Missed a workout—what happens next?” | Give a feasible return step |
| Ask for the next problem | “Timing, or finding a place to walk?” | Gather specific editorial demand without claiming prevalence |

Keep an unscheduled alternate hook for every episode. Select the published version in advance. An alternate in a script file is not a second calendar commitment.

## 9. Writing and episode specification

### Six-beat script contract

| Beat | Job | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Name a recognizable problem or bounded answer | Does the rest of the clip keep its promise? |
| Answer | State the useful principle early | Can a beginner understand it without specialist vocabulary? |
| Example | Show one concrete application | Is an illustration clearly separated from measured evidence? |
| Boundary | Prevent the consequential overgeneralization | Is it spoken or otherwise accessible where necessary? |
| Action | Give one feasible next step | Can the viewer actually identify what to do? |
| Close | Ask a relevant process/constraint question | Would a reply teach us something useful? |

The voice recording determines final timing. A word-count estimate is a planning aid. Never fix a crowded script by speeding up the approved voice or hiding a qualification in unreadable text.

### Exact current example: BP04

**Title:** A short walk can fit into your real week.  
**Alternate, unscheduled:** Where could a short walk fit into your week?  
**Claim type:** official adult guidance plus an editorial planning example.  
**Source IDs:** BPF01 for guidance; BPD02 for community demand.

1. “A short walk can have a place in your week.”
2. “Adult activity guidance lets you build movement in smaller chunks.”
3. “Try planning a familiar route after lunch, with an indoor backup.”
4. “That example is a starting point, not the whole activity target.”
5. “Choose a time and route that fit your current ability.”
6. “What gets in the way: timing, or a place to walk?”

The diagram shows a route and return, with an explicit planning-example label. The comprehension check asks for the main point, next action and limitation. A critical error is concluding that any short walk satisfies all adult activity needs or that one dose applies to every age.

### Canonical episode fields

The V5 calendar stores stable episode ID, week/day/date/platforms, cluster, hook assignment, title, unscheduled alternate, community/evidence/creator IDs, selection rationale, claim type, status, six narration/visual beats, caption, CTA, comprehension answer and critical error, age scope, series and voice-reuse identity. Production layout data lives separately in `production.json`.

For another domain add its equivalent scope fields. Do not carry a fitness age disclaimer into unrelated advice as a generic substitute for understanding the claim. Generate readable scripts, calendars and source maps from canonical data to avoid divergent editable copies.

## 12. Quality assurance and actual verification

### The reusable QA ladder

1. **Evidence:** check the actual source, scope, claim and visible implication.
2. **Script:** one answer, accurate hook, appropriate boundary and usable action.
3. **Identity:** bind script, voice, alignment, config, fonts, renderer and output hashes.
4. **Media:** decode the whole file; inspect dimensions, frame count, duration, codecs and audio.
5. **Captions/layout:** preserve wording, reading time, non-overlap, text bounds and contrast.
6. **Human review:** watch and listen to the complete exact export; inspect representative frames and transitions.
7. **Platform preview:** inspect covers, captions and overlays in the intended uploader.
8. **Audience comprehension:** ask a real target viewer for the takeaway, action and limitation.
9. **Operational review:** confirm cost, hands-on effort, account, schedule and actual publication receipt.

Passing one level does not imply passing the later levels. A model's claim review, an ASR match, a local checkbox and a viewer's trust rating each answer different questions.

### Questions need different designs

| Question | Current or proposed design | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |

### First-exposure research rules

Show one assigned version before the comparison view. Ask the unaided takeaway, action and limitation before preference or explaining the hypothesis. Use neutral labels. Preserve assignment order, exact file hashes, eligibility, starts, skips, playback failures and missing responses. A later side-by-side preference is informed comparative feedback, not independent first exposure.

Small screens are useful for finding confusing wording and unusable layouts. They do not provide stable demographic effects, represent the whole market or establish a reliable small uplift. Repeated consequential misunderstanding merits repair even when average preference is favorable.

### Current citation protocol CV2

- Topic BP03; label: “Schoenfeld et al., 2016 · 21 trained men.”
- Arms: full references in description alone versus the same references plus on-screen label.
- Same narration, spoken qualification, captions, runtime, layout and graphics; the label band is the intended difference.
- Proposed 24–36 adult beginners aged 18–35 across genders, at most six months of consistent resistance training.
- Balanced random assignment in blocks of two; one version first.
- Ask main point, application, whether it proves a universal rest target, trust in another explanation (1–7 or cannot tell), clarity, then source recall.
- Primary item is the trust question; comprehension and overgeneralization are essential safeguards in interpretation.
- Report distributions, counts and missingness. Custom questions are not a validated psychological scale.

### D testing recommendation for a later revision

The existing owner comparison can assess aesthetics. A future audience screen should compare the graphics baseline against a defined D implementation with the same answer, voice, sources and duration. If the new version also changes the hook, scene setting and layout, call it a **creative-package comparison**, not a test isolating character presence.

Measure unaided comprehension, next action, perceived relevance, attainable aspiration, distraction and voluntary interest in another explanation. Use actual instrumented playback if measuring viewing; no such telemetry collector is bundled today. Do not relabel moderator impressions as completion data. Set a sample, stopping rule and decision criterion before recruitment. Recruitment itself has not been authorized or started by this handoff.

### Protect the organic pilot

Publish one version per episode. Keep the style, voice, posting slot and main format stable during a cohort. Retain low-performing posts and record interventions. Different topics, dates and audiences mean organic views are not randomized A/B outcomes. Cross-posting to two platforms is not random assignment. Do not combine a character test, source-label test, new voice and new hook in one result and attribute the outcome to one factor.

## 14. Measurement and go/no-go decisions

### Observation windows

Capture 24-hour diagnostics separately from the primary **first 168 hours**. The evaluator requires timezone-aware publication/observation timestamps, a source-export reference, `reporting_window: first_168_hours`, and collection within a 24-hour grace period after that window. Export the actual first-seven-day window; a later lifetime total is not equivalent.

Keep YouTube and Instagram separate. Preserve platform-native definitions. Engaged views, play-start views, reach, stayed-to-watch and average percentage viewed are not interchangeable. Average percentage viewed can exceed 100% through replaying. Missing values remain null/blank, never invented zeros. Do not sum overlapping lifetime snapshots.

### Frozen operating hurdles for this fitness pilot

| Gate | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Release coverage | At least 12 mature YouTube releases |
| Exposure coverage | At least eight with 100 or more engaged views |
| Attention | Eligible-post median stayed-to-watch ≥60% and median average percentage viewed ≥70% |
| Usefulness | At least five distinct substantive responders |
| Comprehension | At least ten target-viewer checks; ≥80% pass |
| Error review | No unresolved recurring critical misunderstanding |
| Sustainability | Two representative four-video production/revision batches, each within 12 hands-on hours |

These are owner/project operating hurdles chosen before results. They are not industry benchmarks, a power calculation or validated targets for a new domain. Freeze replacements before a new project's first post.

A useful response describes a relevant problem, intended concrete application or reported use. Generic praise, bots, your own accounts and duplicate replies do not count. Keep corrections and substantive disagreement visible. Deduplicate within the observed scope; do not infer that unidentified users across platforms are the same person.

### Decision logic

- **GO_BOUNDED_MONTH_TWO:** all coverage, attention, usefulness, comprehension and production gates pass, with no unresolved recurring critical misunderstanding. Continue for one bounded month.
- **ITERATE:** mixed evidence or one repairable clarity/production problem. Name the change and a fixed follow-up horizon.
- **NO_GO_CURRENT_FORMAT:** adequate observations show both weak attention and weak usefulness, or production remains over capacity after one documented simplification. This rejects the current format, not every possible content business in the domain.
- **INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE:** exposure, mature observations, valid metrics, comprehension or labor are missing. Allow at most one additional 14-day observation/distribution window under the current protocol, then make an explicit investment decision.

The evaluator prioritizes missing evidence before declaring failure. Its boolean gate fields may be false when data are absent; the decision and `missing` list must be read together. Current empty data do not show that the content performed poorly.

### Records to retain

**Observation:** episode/platform/publication ID, version/hash, published/observed timestamps, reporting window, duration, views, engaged views, stayed-to-watch, average duration/percentage viewed, reach, saves, shares, subscribers where available, source export, missing-value reason, traffic/distribution notes.

**Viewer test:** pseudonymous participant ID, eligibility, assignment, exposure sequence, exact media hash, takeaway, action, limitation, pass/critical-error coding, optional ratings, playback issues. Keep contact and consent records outside Git.

**Response coding:** episode/platform, stable anonymous responder token, date, paraphrase, category, usefulness rationale and deduplication scope.

**Labor:** date, batch, task, start/end, active minutes, unattended minutes, revision reason and source record. **Cost:** provider, request ID, accepted/rejected output, actual charge when known, currency, failed/unknown attempts and reviewer time.

- An entire month can be produced with deterministic diagrams and reusable narration/assets.
