# Science, priorities and a real life

Updated 23 October 2026 following the owner's clarification. This is the current naming brief. The earlier Offscreen Athletic, Minor Upgrades and Active Lore concepts remain explorations; no final name has been selected.

## The brand promise

**Science-backed fitness, focused on what matters, built around real life.**

Short expression: **What works. What matters. What fits.**

The audience wants to make worthwhile progress while managing work, study, relationships, budget, access and enjoyment. The brand helps them choose what deserves attention and turn it into something practical. It can cover strength, cardio, food, sleep, recovery and habits.

The name does not have to communicate all three ideas by itself. Use the name for recognition, a clear descriptor for the category, and the content to demonstrate the promise. Internet humor can remain part of the voice; the owner's three principles now lead the positioning.

| Owner's requirement | Meaning in this brand | Visible proof in the content |
|---|---|---|
| Science backed | Explain the best relevant evidence and its limits | Claim-specific sources, plain explanations, uncertainty and corrections |
| 80/20 / Pareto | Identify the important starting decisions before optional refinements | Explain priorities, likely tradeoffs, prerequisites and what can reasonably wait |
| Fits a lifestyle | Adapt the practical example to real constraints and preferences | Examples with time, cost, access and a workable fallback; one clear next action |

Use Pareto as an editorial prioritization principle. Do not turn it into a blanket promise of “80% of fitness results from 20% of the effort.” ASQ describes Pareto analysis in terms of identifying and ranking important contributors; it is not evidence for a universal exercise-results ratio. The transfer to our editorial process is a design choice. [ASQ glossary](https://asq.org/quality-resources/quality-glossary), [ASQ overview](https://asq.org/quality-resources/benchmarking/pareto-analysis?id=01cb52029dea497aab7c5ccc6007d72d)

“Science backed” is a standard for the work, not a decorative claim. Separate researched guidance from an illustrative schedule or an editorial checklist. A citation alone does not establish that an entire branded method has been tested.

## Eight naming ideas

Creative judgments below are hypotheses. Preliminary public-web searches are recorded later in this document; none of these names is confirmed available or cleared.

| Name | Why it fits this brief | Possible tagline | Main tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Essential Effort** | Focuses on the effort worth making. Flexible across fitness activities and ordinary life; the descriptor can make the evidence promise explicit. | Science-backed fitness for real life. | Can sound like work or productivity without the fitness descriptor |
| **Reason & Routine** | Connects the explanation behind a decision with putting it into practice repeatedly. Broadens the original “Rep & Reason” idea. | What works, made doable. | Prioritization is less obvious in the name; “routine” could feel rigid |
| **The Useful Few** | Expresses selecting a small number of worthwhile priorities; gives the Pareto idea a memorable phrase. | The fitness essentials worth your effort. | Abstract without a category descriptor; must not suggest one fixed list works for everyone |
| **Fit by Reason** | Names fitness directly and signals thoughtful choices. Leaves room for training, food, cardio and recovery. | What works. What matters. What fits. | More rational than playful; test whether it sounds natural aloud |
| **Fit in Practice** | Emphasizes advice that survives contact with someone's actual week. | Fitness science for your actual week. | Descriptive and potentially less distinctive; may sound like a professional practice |
| **Effort Well Spent** | Frames time and energy as valuable, with fitness decisions that deserve them. | Put your energy into what matters. | Broad lifestyle/productivity associations; needs fitness and evidence in the bio |
| **Vital Few Fitness** | The most explicit connection to Pareto's important-few concept, with an immediate category cue. | Focus on the fitness that matters most. | “Vital Few” already has multiple business uses; can sound like a consultancy |
| **The Everyday 20** | A more playful numerical reference to the owner's 80/20 philosophy, connected to daily life. | Fitness essentials. Real-life routines. | Easy to misread as 20 minutes daily or a quantified results promise; reserve option only |

## Recommended order for exploration

**Essential Effort is my lead creative recommendation.** It expresses the central value exchange: spend effort on what is worth doing. It can feel energetic and capable in the existing Training Club palette. The scientific standard is expressed clearly in the tagline and earned through the content.

**Reason & Routine is the strongest alternative if trust and explanation should lead.** It connects evidence and behavior in two ordinary words. The brand would need flexible examples so “routine” means something repeatable rather than a rigid lifestyle.

**The Useful Few is the strongest alternative if prioritization should lead.** It can become an editorial identity: we examine a confusing topic, identify the important choices, and show how to use them. Its fitness descriptor should remain visible during launch.

Fit by Reason is the clearest category-first reserve. Fit in Practice and Effort Well Spent emphasize lifestyle compatibility. Vital Few Fitness and The Everyday 20 make the Pareto reference more explicit but introduce more naming or comprehension baggage.

These recommendations concern positioning and creative fit. No audience test establishes a winner.

## A complete example: Essential Effort

**Display name:** Essential Effort | Fitness Explained

**Primary tagline:** Science-backed fitness for real life.

**Brand line:** What works. What matters. What fits.

**Draft bio:** Science-backed fitness. The essentials for strength, cardio, food & recovery—built around real life. Sources included.

**Voice:** informed, practical, encouraging, occasionally funny about ordinary constraints. Explain the tradeoff without framing the audience as lazy or uninformed. Avoid claiming that every problem has one shortcut.

**Visual direction:** retain warm white, ink, cobalt and lime from Training Club. Use one clear explanation, a compact evidence cue and a visible next step. These are brand concepts, not a reason to add an artificial scientist character, lab coat, fake study chart or body score.

Three recurring editorial questions:

1. **What works?** What does the evidence support, for whom, and with what uncertainty?
2. **What matters?** What deserves attention first for this goal and situation?
3. **What fits?** How could the viewer apply it within a realistic week?

Each video can answer one of these questions well; it need not cram three sections into every Short. Across the month, all three must be visible.

Example topics to develop—not new evidence-backed scripts:

- “Your plan has six gym days. Your calendar has two.” A realistic scheduling decision, using B08's existing boundaries.
- “Does protein powder belong on your starting list?” A priority decision, using B09's existing dietary qualifications.
- “What should you ask before your first run-club visit?” A practical cardio/community question requiring its own research.
- “Which part of your routine is hardest to repeat?” A useful community question without an invented physiological claim.

## Validation update

Use the existing [testing plan](TEST_PLAN.md), starting with Essential Effort, Reason & Routine and The Useful Few in identical neutral typography. Keep name-only and name-plus-descriptor results separate. Ask what the viewer thinks is being promised before revealing this brief.

Add three comprehension checks: does the concept communicate credible fitness advice, prioritization and a place in ordinary life? Specifically record interpretations such as effortless results, a guaranteed numerical shortcut, compulsory daily training or advanced-athlete-only content. Avoid suggesting these answers in the initial questions. Ask preference last.

Do not change the name and test a new hook style in the same comparison. Keep the current content's claim boundaries, approved voice and no-character decision. Selecting and screening a name comes before a production-wide rename.
