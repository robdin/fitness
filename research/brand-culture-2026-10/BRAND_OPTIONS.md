# Three brand directions to test

> Earlier exploration. The owner's subsequent science / 80–20 / lifestyle clarification is captured in [the current positioning and naming brief](POSITIONING_AND_NAMES.md), which now leads name selection. None of the concepts below was selected.

Prepared 23 October 2026. Proposed names and creative directions; none selected, cleared or audience-validated. These extend the owner's Training Club choice and no-character decision. Keep the approved narration voice.

## Shared positioning

Useful, evidence-aware fitness explanations for people building an active life. Begin with male beginners aged 16–35, while welcoming different abilities and interests. Strength, cardio, everyday movement, food, recovery and repeatable habits all fit. The brand is an educator; “club” language must not imply facilities, coaching credentials or real events that do not exist.

The culture research suggests potential ways to communicate this promise. It does not establish that male viewers share a gaming identity or prefer a particular color. Adult audience tests must include non-gamers and viewers who do not already follow fitness creators.

## Offscreen Athletic — lead creative recommendation

**Idea:** fitness becomes valuable in the life you live after the video ends.

**Tagline:** Fitness for the life you actually live.

**Display name:** Offscreen Athletic | Fitness Explained

**Draft bio:** Strength, cardio, food & recovery. Clear answers. Small next steps. Sources included.

**Cultural connection:** real-world activity and social participation; an internet-aware name without a fleeting catchphrase. See C03, C07 and C13 in the [register](SOURCE_REGISTER.md). The name is our invention from that territory, not a phrase proven popular in those sources.

**Personality:** capable, warm, observant; lightly funny about daily friction. Aspirational through capability and participation rather than a particular physique.

**Visual treatment:** an oversized two-line wordmark; warm white background, ink type, cobalt fixture labels, lime action highlights. A repeatable five-item subject strip—strength / cardio / food / recovery / habits—makes the breadth visible. Use equipment, maps, checklists and food objects. No avatar or mandatory human face.

**Series:** “Side Quest” for trying an activity; “The Useful Bit” for one practical explanation; “Offscreen Notes” for logs, follow-ups and corrections. Series names are descriptive proposals, not separately cleared marks.

**Voice examples:** “Your plan has six gym days. Your calendar has two.” / “First run-club visit: what do you ask before you go?” / “A missed Tuesday needs a next step.”

**Main tradeoff:** “offscreen” may be read as digital detox, while “athletic” may sound advanced. The bio and first three posts must make beginner fitness obvious. Existing Offscreen clothing/digital-wellness uses make the root less distinctive than a completely invented word. Test the whole name.

## Minor Upgrades — playful progress

**Idea:** a useful fitness change can be ordinary and still worth making.

**Tagline:** Small changes. More capable.

**Display name:** Minor Upgrades | Everyday Fitness

**Draft bio:** Fitness, one useful update at a time. Strength, cardio, food & recovery—with the reasons attached.

**Cultural connection:** gaming updates, self-aware ambition and recurring progress. C02, C08 and C17 motivate the territory; they do not validate this name.

**Personality:** understated, witty, practical. The humor concerns the situation, never someone's body or beginner status.

**Visual treatment:** large editorial “update” cards inside the existing palette. Cobalt headings, warm-paper checklists and lime action tabs. Version numbers identify episodes, not quantified improvements in the viewer's health. Do not draw an invented “body XP” score or percentage increase.

**Series:** “Patch Notes” for one habit adjustment; “Optional Extras” for equipment/supplement questions; “Still Counts” for realistic participation.

**Voice examples:** “Protein powder: optional add-on?” / “Your routine got another update. Your log is confused.” / “Today's update: a backup time.”

**Main tradeoff:** it can sound like software, home improvement or low ambition. The name alone is weak at signaling fitness; keep the descriptor prominent. “Minor” refers to the size of the change, not the viewer's age. Avoid wording that implies training automatically repairs a broken person.

## Active Lore — culturally expressive education

**Idea:** explain the stories, rules and assumptions people encounter around fitness.

**Tagline:** Know the why. Try the next thing.

**Display name:** Active Lore | Fitness Explained

**Draft bio:** The why behind training, cardio, food & recovery. Real questions. Clear explanations. Sources included.

**Cultural connection:** shared references, community questions and participatory explanation. C01, C02 and C12 inform the territory; “lore” appears widely beyond fitness, so comprehension must be tested.

**Personality:** curious and conversational; an informed member of the discussion rather than a gatekeeper.

**Visual treatment:** bold question-first covers; cobalt sidebar, warm-paper answer and an ink source cue. Lime marks the practical next step. Thread-like graphics must use “Example question” unless a real attributed/permissioned question is being shown. The graphic cannot impersonate a real Reddit or Instagram screenshot.

**Series:** “Fitness Lore” for investigating a common belief; “Explain This” for terminology; “Try Something” for activity exploration.

**Voice examples:** “Who decided soreness was a score?” / “Your first run club: what does ‘no-drop’ mean?” / “Which fitness rule have you heard three different versions of?”

**Main tradeoff:** “lore” could imply rumor, fantasy or gaming content. An evidence-aware channel must demonstrate the distinction between a story people repeat and a claim supported by a source. The name may be less clear to older or less online viewers.

## Breadth check: the same pillars in each direction

These are original creative prompts, not new evidence-backed scripts or promises about health effects.

| Pillar | Offscreen Athletic | Minor Upgrades | Active Lore |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | Your first session, with a plan. | First-session patch notes. | Translate the workout plan. |
| Cardio / walking | First run club? Ask this first. | New side quest: the local run club. | What does “no-drop” actually mean? |

| Recovery | What happens between sessions? | Recovery belongs in the update. | Why do rest days feel like cheating? |

## Brand architecture and production rules

Keep one master brand across Instagram and YouTube. The display descriptor can explain the category; the public identity should not depend on different names per platform. Handles shown during exploration are hypothetical until checked in the account interfaces. Do not reserve accounts or buy domains as part of this research.

Reuse warm white `#F5F3EA`, ink `#14221C`, lime `#CAEF51`, cobalt `#2149CA` and Space Grotesk from [V4](../../brand/v4/STYLE_GUIDE.md). Different territories change composition and voice before changing the entire production system. Keep claim-specific source cues, readable captions and the explanatory diagram as the main object.

Start with one cultural reference in at most one of the four weekly videos. Deliver the useful answer immediately after the hook. Maintain the source boundaries already established for adult versus youth guidance. A cultural reference never substitutes for evidence or alters a medical qualification.

**Recommended next decision:** test these three names with the same neutral design and descriptor first. Then compare their full creative treatments. That prevents a preferred layout from being mistaken for a preferred name.
