# Testing cultural relevance without confusing attention with success

Proposed 23 October 2026. No participants recruited, messages sent, media published or experiments run. The existing go/no-go priorities remain audience interest and a sustainable process capped at **12 hands-on hours per week**. This plan adds brand/culture learning; it does not replace the established month-one decision rules.

**Current creative brief:** [Body of our dreams · dreamy but tactical](../../brand/dreamy-tactical/BRAND_DIRECTION.md). Dreams in Practice, Built into Life and Everyday Becoming are candidates to explore, not a locked or cleared test set. The earlier name stimuli are superseded. Test the mission's meaning before selecting names; avoid asking people to judge everything in one long session.

## 0. Does the aspiration communicate the intended promise?

Recruit an exploratory sample of **18 adult fitness beginners across genders**, aiming for nine aged 18–24 and nine aged 25–35, with varied work/study/care responsibilities and gym/non-gym interests. This revises the earlier male-only recruitment plan to match the owner's inclusive vision. It is still a small convenience sample; do not compare demographic subgroups as if powered for such conclusions. Record useful context without unnecessary health/body details. No recruitment has happened.

Show the headline “Your dream body. Your real life.” with the same fitness descriptor in a neutral treatment. Before explaining the founder story, ask:

1. What do you think this account would help you with?
2. What does “dream body” mean here, in your own words?
3. Who do you think this is for? What gave you that impression?
4. What would you expect to learn next?

Then show the founder story and the same five topic examples. Ask what changed, whether the promise feels encouraging or pressuring, and why. Keep pre-story and post-story answers separate. Do not lead the first response with our preferred interpretation of look/feel/move.

Code observations as self-defined goal, single prescribed physique, guaranteed/rapid transformation, broad fitness, or sleep/beauty/general self-help confusion; allow multiple codes and preserve verbatim responses with consent. Two independent instances of the same consequential misunderstanding trigger wording review and a fresh check before release. This is a conservative editorial trigger, not a population estimate. Do not treat silence as evidence of safety or broad appeal.

## 1. Name comprehension before aesthetic preference

Once a three-name shortlist is chosen and screened, use **18 fresh adult beginners** with the inclusive recruitment targets above. Fresh participants preserve unaided category and name recall after the mission study. If recruiting a second sample is impractical, use the first sample for informed preferences only and explicitly drop the unaided-comprehension claim. Budget recruitment and review time; these are proposed stages, not free work outside the 12-hour ceiling.

Assign six participants to each first-seen name, balancing age within groups. Show one name in a neutral, identical type treatment for five seconds; hide it. Ask, in order:

1. What kind of account do you expect this to be?
2. What would you expect to get from following it?
3. What name do you remember? How would you spell it?
4. Does it remind you of an existing account or business? Which one?

Next show the same category descriptor and the same five example topics under that name. Ask whether it fits walking, strength, food and recovery, and whether a beginner belongs. Then show the other two names in counterbalanced order. Ask preference last, with reasons. Record the first-seen results separately from comparison responses.

Do not combine name-only and name-plus-descriptor comprehension into one score. Do not describe a participant as “confused” merely for disliking a name. Code literal misunderstandings separately, including sleep/dream journaling, beauty-only, general self-help, or advanced-athletes-only.

**Practical decision rules, set before recruitment:** repeated same-category confusion in at least two of six first exposures requires a revision; a distinct name recalled and interpreted accurately by at least four of six merits further exploration. These are small-sample editorial gates, not significance tests or industry benchmarks. Report counts and actual explanations, not a synthetic 0–100 brand score. A close result stays inconclusive.

The initial test does not validate ages 16–17. If teen-specific launch decisions depend on preferences, design a suitable separate consented test. Do not infer teen response from the adult sample.

## 3. Test the cultural opening against plain language

Begin with three suitable episodes and two versions per episode. Change only the first spoken sentence and its matching opening graphic. Keep runtime close, answer onset comparable, and the remaining narration, caption wording, source cue, CTA and audio mix identical. Avoid B03, which already carries the citation-visibility experiment.

| Test topic | Plain opening | Cultural opening | What could go wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein powder / B09 | “Do you need protein powder to start?” | “Protein powder: optional add-on?” | Viewers infer that all dietary needs are optional |
| First walking plan / proposed P-MOVE-01 | “How would a walk fit into your week?” | “Side quest: make one walk fit your actual week.” | The metaphor distracts from planning or implies a challenge target |
| Missed session / B16 | “Missed a workout—what happens next?” | “Missed Tuesday. The plan needs an update.” | A software metaphor creates confusion or sounds judgmental |

First do a private comprehension screen. Each participant sees only one version of a given topic, counterbalanced across people; otherwise they may remember the answer from the first version. Ask the takeaway and what the video does **not** establish. Repeated material misunderstanding blocks release of the wording even if preference is high.

For an organic launch, assign planned cultural/plain treatments before posting and rotate topic/week positions. Compare matched episodes at the same 24-hour and 7-day age. This is a noisy observational comparison: topics, initial distribution, audience composition and timing vary. Cross-posting to two platforms is not random assignment, and posting duplicate videos sequentially is not a clean A/B test. Use a genuinely randomized creative test only if the chosen platform/tool supports it and the user later authorizes that activity.

## 4. Record interest, understanding and production effort

| Outcome | Observation | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Initial attention | Native Shorts stayed-to-watch; Reels early retention where available | Did the opening earn attention? Do not substitute a different view metric |
| Sustained attention | Native average percentage viewed or watch time with duration; retention shape | Did people stay for the explanation? Loops can inflate averages |
| Practical value | Saves/shares, useful follow-up questions, repeated requests for a topic | Show counts and the available denominator; comments may include disagreement |
| Brand fit | Unaided category, recall, willingness to view another relevant topic | A preference is not a subscription or behavior change |
| Accuracy | Wrong-takeaway count and source misunderstandings | A failed explanation cannot win on retention alone |
| Sustainability | Human minutes researching, writing, reviewing, revising, publishing and responding | Separate from unattended rendering and initial setup |

Keep YouTube and Instagram denominators separate. Never pool views, reach and engaged views into a single rate. Where supported, report saves/shares per 1,000 accounts reached; show raw counts and mark a metric missing when the denominator is unavailable. Existing Metricool access does not guarantee every native retention metric is exposed; native Studio/Insights may be required.

Use medians and individual post results for this small month. Do not announce a format winner because of one outlier. As an operational rule, require a favorable direction across at least three topic comparisons, no recurring critical misunderstandings and acceptable labor before giving a cultural format more slots. Three comparisons still do not establish causality. Low reach or conflicting outcomes mean “insufficient evidence,” not “no audience interest.”

## 5. A repeatable culture scan within the 12-hour ceiling

Suggested recurring budget: **1h listening + 2h evidence/writing + 2h production/revisions + 2h review/accessibility + 1h scheduling + 1h community/measurement + 1h analysis + 2h buffer = 12h**. These are planning allocations, not measured production times. Initial name research, audience recruitment and five new episodes must be logged separately and included in the launch workload, not hidden as free setup.

Inside the one-hour listening budget:

- 20 minutes: r/Fitness and r/running questions, plus one adjacent activity/food community. Look for concrete recurring questions, not only top-voted posts.
- 15 minutes: native Instagram/TikTok/YouTube discovery when access permits. Save the actual URL, creator, publication date, format and fitness connection.
- 10 minutes: native X searches/lists if available. Until then record the access gap; do not fill it with a fabricated trend summary.
- 10 minutes: one current meme chronology and a counterexample/backlash check.
- 5 minutes: choose one testable idea or explicitly choose no new meme this week.

Discovery terms: “first gym,” “run club,” “walking,” “routine confusing,” “rest day,” “meal prep,” plus the current candidate phrase. The Reddit Pro documentation in C19 may help a future configured workflow; no monitoring service has been created here.

For each candidate record: URL; observed date; publication/event date; geography if known; platform; observed joke/situation; audience relevance; counterexamples; intended useful answer; access limits; reuse/asset needs; hours estimate; plain fallback; decision.

Require two separately originating recent examples before calling a phrase a live cross-community opportunity. A meme article plus the one post it describes counts as one origin. This is an editorial screening rule, not a statistical trend definition. One excellent community question can still justify an evergreen explainer without being called a trend.

## 6. Month-one decision

Preserve the go/no-go thresholds; new BP-series and CV2 identifiers isolate the selected Blueprint cohort, while prior identifiers remain historical. Add cultural treatment, selected brand version and actual hands-on minutes to the readout. Do not pool the new identity with prior graphics versions without labeling the change.

Continue a treatment when interest is repeated, viewers understand the lesson, the broad content promise is credible and the process fits the time cap. Revise it when the humor attracts attention but obscures the answer or costs too much. Retire a meme when references need explanation, behavior is mainly corrective outrage, or maintaining freshness consumes the buffer. Keep a clear evergreen fallback ready.
