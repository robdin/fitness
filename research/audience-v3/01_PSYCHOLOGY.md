# Questions that earn attention and a useful response

The useful psychological promise is **“I can make sense of this, and I can do the next step.”** Our working hypothesis is that concrete uncertainty plus an achievable answer will serve this audience better than status anxiety. This is a design hypothesis informed by research, not a measured audience result.

## What the evidence actually supports

Curiosity can support learning, but the famous curiosity findings include laboratory trivia tasks, not scrolling fitness feeds. The information gap must be recognizable: “What does 3 × 8 mean?” gives a novice a clear missing piece. “The secret nobody tells you” gives them almost no reason to trust the promise. Headline research also finds that the value of concreteness depends on the starting headline; neither maximum mystery nor maximum detail always wins. [Gruber et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25284006/), [Le Quéré and Matias](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-81575-9)

Exercise motivation research gives a reason to support competence and choice. We can explain the decision, offer a manageable action and let the viewer fit it to their situation. This does not demonstrate that our videos will improve adherence. It gives us a better question to test: can the viewer identify an action they would actually take? [Teixeira et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/1479-5868-9-78)

Question asking has been associated with liking and perceived responsiveness in live conversation, particularly when people ask follow-up questions. That is a reason to listen to replies, not proof that placing a question mark in a caption increases reach. The 2025 correction reports minor reporting errors and continued support for the conclusions; retain that qualification. [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28447835/), [correction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40111841/)

Sharing research suggests a role for useful and emotionally activating material. Our application is useful surprise, recognition and relief: a confusing instruction becomes readable, or a missed session becomes a solvable scheduling problem. Outrage and shame are avoidable editorial choices. An experiment with 223 men found adverse body-image responses to fitspiration images relative to neutral images; this strengthens the case for showing skills and decisions instead of using ideal physiques as the pressure mechanism. [Sharing research](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1509/jmr.10.0353), [male image-exposure experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32977202/)

## A question taxonomy for this channel

| Question job | Original example | Likely value to test | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decode something visible | “What does 3 × 8 actually mean?” | Immediate comprehension | Explaining five terms at once |
| Resolve a choice | “Two gym days—where do you start?” | Reduced decision effort | Pretending two days is optimal for everyone |
| Correct a mistaken signal | “Not sore—did the workout count?” | Relief plus a better progress measure | Diagnosing pain in comments |
| Invite prediction | “Same weight, one extra rep: progress?” | Active inspection of an example | Showing invented results as research data |
| Name a barrier | “What feels less clear: the plan or the machines?” | Specific editorial demand | A forced binary treated as a complete survey |
| Ask about a process | “What do you record after a set?” | Relevant, low-disclosure responses | Collecting body measurements or health histories |
| Follow up thoughtfully | “You mentioned setup—seat height or the controls?” | Responsiveness | Pretending a canned answer is personal coaching |
| Elicit transfer | “What would you do differently next visit?” | Practical understanding | Counting any comment as evidence of benefit |

These are original prompts, not quotations from research participants. Use one principal question per post. Offer concrete options when helpful, but let a viewer describe a different barrier. A poll is directional feedback from respondents, not population prevalence.

## Engagement with consequences

The hook creates the question; the body earns the answer; the closing prompt asks for a relevant observation. Avoid withholding the answer until a follow, manufacturing disagreement, asking viewers to tag an insecure friend, or treating “comment YES” as proof of usefulness. A save-worthy checklist should remain useful without saving it. A share should help someone, not embarrass them.

Measure separately: attention (did they stay?), comprehension (what did they understand?), usefulness (what action or question did they express?), and sustainability (what did it take to produce?). A confused argument can inflate comments while failing the product. Count distinct substantive responses with a coding rule; keep disagreement, corrections, generic praise and spam separate.

## Applied to production

B01 reduces first-visit uncertainty; B04 decodes notation; B07 invites comparison; B06 removes a misleading soreness signal; B16 supplies a recovery from a missed plan. Every episode has an early answer, one explanatory object, a bounded action and a comprehension question. The exact speed, graphics and question-versus-answer opening remain testable editorial choices. No laboratory study has validated this complete package.
