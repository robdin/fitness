# Integrating the research into a full month

The prior N-series starts with credible training debates but assumes too much beginner literacy. The B-series starts with entering the gym, reading a routine and learning the setup, then moves into recovery, comparison and repeatability. This is a strategic revision based on desk research; it is not evidence that V2 failed with viewers. Neither series has live audience observations.

## What changed and why

| Perspective | Production decision | Where to inspect it | How it can fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Psychology | Small recognizable problem, early answer, achievable action | B01, B04, B16 | Viewers still cannot state a next step |
| Video creation | Larger explanatory objects, fewer words per script, changes tied to meaning | B04 grouping, B07 comparison, B03 timer | Animation distracts or captions outrun reading |
| Beginner journey | Orientation and terminology before optimization | Weeks 1–2 | Sampled needs do not match the people reached |
| Brand | Three visual packages; owner selected Club | B01 brand comparison | Looks generic, intimidating or untrustworthy |
| Writing | Specific tension and a bounded payoff | All 16 scripts and unscheduled hook alternatives | A hook creates a broader claim than the answer supports |

## Validation ladder

**1. Desk validation — completed for this revision:** recorded sources and their access limits; separate community demand from scientific support; attach claim boundaries to each script; check adult versus youth scope. This is an editorial source review, not a new systematic review or qualified personal-training assessment.

**2. Artifact validation — recorded in the production receipt:** verify exact script/audio hashes, provider alignment, captions, text bounds, contrast pairs, dimensions, codecs, duration, full decode and archive integrity. Independent local speech recognition flags possible wording issues; it cannot certify pronunciation or replace final listening. Inspect representative stills and browser playback. Checks must describe what was actually run.

**3. Target-viewer validation — not yet conducted:** small first-exposure brand and comprehension screens with adult beginners. Use the actual final export, not just the script. Collect the unaided takeaway, action and limitation before preference. No fabricated participants, scores or quotes. Recruitment is a separate user-authorized action.

**4. Public pilot — not yet launched:** 16 core posts across four weeks, cross-posted to two platforms; four carousels and eight stories. November 1–30 is proposed, with the last core post November 28. December 5 completes its seven-day observation. Shift the whole schedule if release is delayed. No upload or scheduling has been performed.

**5. Business decision:** interest and sustainable production, not virality as a single number. Use the frozen [protocol](../../experiments/month-one-v3.json), not a retrospective best-looking metric.

## Experiment sequence and stop rules

The project owner selected Training Club. Before release, the optional audience screen can compare the three B01 brand packages for clarity and fit. The first-exposure assignment is balanced and random; later side-by-side preference is a separate question. Repeated material misunderstanding in two people triggers repair. The small sample chooses a usable direction; it does not estimate a reliable retention uplift. A separate B07 motion/static comparison can test whether the visual relationship is understood. Keep the scripts and audio identical for that comparison.

During release, eight question openings and eight answer openings are balanced by topic cluster, week and weekday. Different topics, exposures and audiences remain confounds. Report each group’s counts, duration and exposure spread. Do not describe the descriptive difference as “questions increase reach by X%.” Keep one scheduled version per topic. A Trial Reel, if later used, is logged separately and is not randomized A/B evidence.

At 24 hours, capture diagnostics without calling a winner. At seven days, save the first-168-hour export within a 24-hour collection grace period. Review all posts, including those with little exposure. A minimum of 12 mature releases and eight with at least 100 engaged YouTube views is an operating sufficiency rule, not a statistically powered sample.

The attention hurdles are median stayed-to-watch ≥60% and median average percentage viewed ≥70% on eligible YouTube posts. The usefulness hurdle is five distinct substantive audience responders; generic praise, spam and duplicate replies do not count. The comprehension hurdle is at least ten target-viewer checks with ≥80% passing the episode’s takeaway/action/critical-limit rubric. These numbers are provisional project hurdles retained before results, not claimed industry benchmarks. No recurring unresolved critical misunderstanding may remain.

For sustainability, the latest two representative production weeks must each fit the user’s 12-hour hands-on cap. Count research, review, revisions, posting, community and measurement. Unattended rendering is separate. This one-off rebuild is setup work. **Uploading a prebuilt month does not establish sustainable creation:** measure two real four-video production/revision batches or keep that gate unknown. The weekly budget remains a plan, not measured labor.

GO means one bounded second month when all gates pass. ITERATE means one specified repair with a bounded follow-up. NO-GO rejects this format if adequately observed attention and usefulness both fail, or production stays above capacity after a documented simplification. Missing exposure, comprehension or labor yields INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, with at most one additional 14-day observation/distribution window before an explicit investment decision. Do not quietly lower the hurdles.

## Measurement instruments

Store audience exports with their original platform labels and dates. Use the empty JSON observation file for analysis adapters; existing CSV ingestion is also available. A viewer-test row needs an anonymous participant ID, adult beginner eligibility, assignment/first exposure, exact media hash, takeaway, action, limitation, pass/critical-error coding and optional ratings. Do not collect names, body photos or health histories.

A response-coding row needs episode/platform, anonymous stable responder token, date, category, paraphrase and reason for usefulness coding. Categories: actionable question, reported intended action, reported applied action, correction, relevant disagreement, generic praise, spam. A response may be meaningful without endorsing us. The distinct-useful count deduplicates people within the reviewed scope; cross-platform identity cannot be inferred.

A labor row needs date, batch, task, start/end, active minutes, unattended minutes, revision reason and source/receipt reference. Record actual provider charges separately; a model name containing “free” is not an invoice. The empty files under measurement are intentionally empty, not simulated success data.

## Remaining uncertainty

The design may still feel too diagram-heavy; the questions may attract intermediate lifters instead of beginners; the narration may be too brisk; a new account may get too little distribution to decide. The proposed experiments expose those failures rather than assuming they are solved. Brand preference, real learning, account reach, recurring human labor and provider billing remain unmeasured.
