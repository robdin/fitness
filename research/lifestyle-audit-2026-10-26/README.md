## 2. Mental models, translated into ordinary decisions

| Turuan model | What the audience sees | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| List Price vs. Real Price | Count the whole trip | The calendar includes travel, changing, the actual session and getting home |
| Rocks, Pebbles & Sand | Choose what fits your actual week | Existing work, care and social plans stay visible while choosing suitable training slots |
| Dimmer Switch Mentality | Prepare an appropriate fallback | An after-lunch walk has a weather/access alternative; a strength routine uses its own adaptation guidance |
| One-in-a-Row Principle | Choose the next return | A missed visit becomes a specific next session and one practical adjustment |
| Goodhart's Law | Record information that helps the next decision | Keep setup and performance context; attendance or a log is not a measurement of muscle growth |

Use at most one of these ideas in a short lesson. The production structure is **recognizable situation → useful decision → completed example → one action**. Framework names stay in editorial metadata unless a future audience test shows demand for the concepts themselves.

Turuan's source material is editorial. We have not validated these named models as fitness interventions. Avoid universal 80/20 results, claims that any tiny action preserves all fitness, or promises that a full life always fits if ordered correctly. A fallback can include rescheduling or rest; a short walk is not an equivalent dose of strength training.

The behavioral research also requires restraint. A randomized trial of 877 gym members found that a simple planning prompt did not improve attendance. A review of 13 trials found no statistically significant overall effect in its pooled analysis; a subgroup with reinforcement was more promising. A separate five-week factorial trial suggests that combinations of planning and monitoring can matter, but self-report and substantial dropout limit interpretation. None tests our short videos or proves that a worksheet makes the habit stick. [Carrera et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7872013/) · [Silva et al., 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0206294) · [Schroé et al., 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12966-020-01001-x)

## 3. The beginner's missing steps

The actual scripts show:

- **16/16 have an action beat.** Adding generic calls to action does not solve the main problem.
- **16/16 end with a question.** For practical teaching, a finished example and next step often deserve that final position. The optional conversation prompt can live in the caption or story. The introduction can still end with a follow invitation.
- **8/16 mention a routine.** Several assume the viewer has already selected and understood one.
- The current rendered timelines are approximately **16.7–23.9 seconds**. Six beats plus captions, headings, diagrams and source/scope labels create a tight reading budget. This observation is not a measured retention effect.

The hypothetical beginner's response is: “I understand the advice. Which routine do I use? What do I write down? What does this look like on Tuesday?” This is a simulated perspective, not a participant quote.

| Need | Current friction | Concrete improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | Some hooks announce a broad lesson | Start with a recognizable tension: “Your workout fits the calendar. Does the trip?” |
| Understanding | Graphic labels repeat the narration | Show the actual calendar, message, lunch or recorded machine setting |
| Action | “Follow a routine” and “keep a log” leave prerequisites open | Supply the orientation question and copyable log; develop a checked routine-resource route |
| Continued interest | Individual tips do not explain what help comes next | Provide a Start here path across preparation, first visit, session record and returning |
| Aspiration | Process advice can seem detached from the viewer's appearance goal | Acknowledge how they want to look, feel and move, without a transformation deadline |
| Trust | Practical planning can look like a scientific prescription | Keep claim-specific evidence cues, label invented examples and preserve meaningful limits |

### A useful starting route

**Intro → first-visit question → fit the whole trip → record the session → return after disruption.**

Each episode must still work on its own because feeds are unordered. The route belongs in profile navigation and a real accessible resource. The starter kit is a concrete draft of that resource, but it does not yet supply a vetted selection of complete routines. Its immediate route is asking an appropriate provider about beginner-plan help, the service offered and any fee. Do not advertise a free program or download that does not exist.

Useful experienced-person details to include now: save the equipment identity and settings, check the entire trip, ask a run-club organizer about pace/walking/last-group support, list only missing meal ingredients, and make a specific return plan. These deepen existing material without expanding every clip.

### Formative viewer check — proposed, not conducted

Use the existing adult-beginner research protocol and log, with a small initial group of eight adults who match the beginner/returner audience. This is a usability screen, not a powered estimate of follows, retention or fitness outcomes. Do not recruit anyone or send messages without separate authorization.

- Give four participants the current BP08 first and four LP08 first, using a recorded random assignment if running the comparison. A prototype/render must exist before a video test can run; script-only feedback is a separate method.
- Ask without replay: “What would you do next?” Then ask them to sketch the calendar entry and identify anything still needed. Record whether they account for the whole trip and understand that a suitable routine still supplies the training instructions.
- Ask the existing comprehension question and record consequential misunderstandings. A repeated misunderstanding across participants is a revision signal; any potentially harmful interpretation should be reviewed individually.
- Show the second version only after collecting the first-version response. Ask comparative preference last; label it a carryover-affected preference, not an independent performance result.
- Test the intro separately, after the task comparison, to avoid priming the episode test. Ask who the account is for, what it helps with and what they expect next. This sequence tests orientation after exposure; a true cold-profile test would need fresh participants. If the existing protocol already has separate profile participants, use them rather than creating a competing study.
- A proposed intro clarity screen is at least six of eight identifying busy beginners/returners and at least two content areas. This is an editorial revision threshold, not statistical evidence of success. Record raw counts and examples.

Do not collapse comprehension, stated follow interest, an observed planning task and actual platform follows into one success score. They answer different questions. The intro brief's stand-alone test can be used instead if run first with a fresh group; do not report the same people as independent cold viewers in both tests.
