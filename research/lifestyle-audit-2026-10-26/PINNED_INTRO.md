## Recommendation

Yes: create one short **Start here** video. Its main job is to help a profile visitor answer three questions: **Is this for me? What will I get? Why come back?** It should demonstrate the account's promise with recognizable situations and actual examples, rather than recite a mission statement.

The current sixteen videos answer individual questions. The November 4 founder carousel, BPK01, explains why the brand exists. Neither provides a single video introduction across training, movement, food and recovery. The intro fills that orientation gap; the existing carousel remains useful for the fuller origin story.

This is an editorial hypothesis about clarity, not evidence that an introduction will increase reach, follows or adherence. A warm profile visitor and a cold feed viewer are different contexts. Keep a specific first-frame problem so the video can also make sense in a feed, but evaluate its orientation job separately from topic-video discovery performance.

## What it should communicate

- **Person:** a busy adult starting or restarting fitness. Use the current male beginner creative lens without saying the account is only for men.
- **Goal:** the appearance, health and capability the person wants; no single physique is the universal destination.
- **Method:** research translated into useful priorities and practical examples.
- **Scope:** training, movement, food and recovery, with realistic choices and a route back after interruptions.
- **Reason to return:** one useful next step at a time.

Keep “Blueprint” as the style name. “Body of our dreams” is campaign wording, not a cleared public brand name. Do not call the brand scientifically proven or promise a fixed result from 80/20 effort.

## Main script

Target roughly 30–35 seconds. Timing below is a planning estimate; use relaxed conversational delivery and measure the real audio before animation.

> You want to get in shape. You also have work, friends, and a life.
>
> That's what this account is about.
>
> We take the fitness research and make it useful: workouts that fit your week, food you actually enjoy, and what to do when life gets in the way.
>
> The goal? The body of your dreams, with room for the rest of your life.
>
> Follow along. We'll figure out the next step together.

This is an account promise and an invitation, not an exercise, nutrition or medical prescription. It needs no detached scientific citation attached to the mission itself. If any source-backed teaching excerpt is shown, retain its relevant qualification and source cue; do not cut a numeric claim free of its context. The founder story is omitted from narration for focus and remains in BPK01.

Only three still-companion previews currently exist. A new arrival-at-home scene or character acting sequence is a **proposed asset**, not completed footage. The lower-screen sticker placement from the previews should not simply be carried over as the main introductory treatment. ## Cover and posting copy

**Cover:** `START HERE` / `Dream body. Real life.`

**Title:** `Getting fit when you already have a full life`

**Caption draft:**

> Starting fitness—or starting again—with a full calendar? This account turns research into useful decisions about training, movement, food and recovery. Your goals can include how you look, feel and move. We share practical examples, explain the evidence behind specific claims, and make the next step clear. Follow for fitness that belongs in your real life.

Use one spoken CTA, following. The pinned intro is the exception to the teaching-post rule that should usually end with a doable action. It is an orientation asset. Regular episodes still need to demonstrate the next action; “follow for tips” cannot substitute for that.

Do not direct viewers to an unpublished guide, unavailable downloadable routine or nonexistent personalized service. Once the relevant posts are live, the caption can name a real starting episode. Verify the destination before changing the CTA.

## Evaluation

### Before publishing: does the promise make sense?

Use eight new adult beginners/returners as a small formative screen. Include busy male beginners for the current creative lens and other intended adult viewers so the broader promise is not silently dropped. This is a usability screen, not a representative or powered experiment.

Show the first five seconds once, then ask: “What do you think this account will help you do?” Show the whole video, then ask, without prompting with brand language:

1. Who does this seem to be for?
2. What would you expect the next three posts to cover?
3. What does “body of your dreams” mean here?
4. Would you open another post? Which type, and why?
5. What, if anything, makes this feel unrealistic or less trustworthy?

Record the actual answers and the exact media version. Proposed editorial pass condition: at least six of eight recognize practical fitness for a busy beginner/returner, and at least six can name two relevant subject areas. Any recurring belief that it promises a guaranteed transformation, one mandatory body type or individualized coaching triggers revision. These are local revision rules, not industry benchmarks. Follow intent is weaker evidence than actual behavior and should be labeled accordingly.

### After publication: keep orientation and discovery separate

Take seven-day and twenty-eight-day readouts. Record actual publication time, account, video ID, media hash and what pin/trailer state was confirmed. Review retention/watch-time data and follows or subscribers attributed to the video **only where the platform actually provides those fields**. Do not invent a cross-platform attribution system.

Keep profile/channel visits and account-level follower changes as context. Their ratio is not a clean causal conversion rate: visits may repeat, follows can happen elsewhere and many posts contribute. Do not claim the intro caused account growth from a before/after comparison.

Qualitatively code comments or replies for “this fits my situation,” expected next-topic requests, category confusion and unrealistic-outcome interpretations. Separate those from generic praise or liking D. A strong first evaluation is a clear account promise and a coherent next-post expectation. Viral reach is not required to justify a useful profile introduction.

If comprehension is weak, revise the promise or examples. If comprehension is strong but the opening loses people, test one alternate opening while holding the body and ending fixed in a future private comparison. Do not simultaneously change D, voice, pacing, script and CTA and label it a hook test.
