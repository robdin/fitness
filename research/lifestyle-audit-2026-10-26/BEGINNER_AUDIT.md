# Blueprint month: a beginner's attention and usefulness audit

Prepared 26 October 2026. **Persona review and editorial hypotheses, not viewer feedback or measured performance.**

## Scope and evidence

The requested lens is a new adult male beginner or returner who wants to get fit while working, studying, socializing and managing an ordinary week. This is one segment of the current broader adult ICP; it does not replace the inclusive brand positioning.

Reviewed the complete `content/month-one-v5/calendar.json`, `production.json`, `SCRIPTS.md`, `SUPPORTING_POSTS.md`, `SOURCE_MAP.md`, `brand/blueprint/STYLE_GUIDE.md`, and the ICP and current-state sections of `docs/CONTENT_IDEA_FRAMEWORK_AND_HANDOFF.md`. Visually inspected the existing 16-cover contact sheet, 16-example-scene contact sheet and full-size action frames for BP01, BP11 and BP16. Read all 16 media timelines. This is not a full playback/listening review or an audit of actual social-platform overlays.

No canonical episodes, videos, dates or posting configuration changed. Proposed scientific or exercise-prescription changes below require their own source review before production. Existing source IDs are traceability pointers, not a fresh external verification in this audit.

## The most useful conclusion

The month is considerate and credible, but often gets me **ready to take an action without showing me how to complete it**. If I were this beginner, I could understand the advice and still leave thinking: “Okay, but which routine? What do I write down? What does this look like on Tuesday?”

The solution is not to add a CTA to every video. They already have them:

- **16 of 16** episodes contain an explicit action beat.
- **16 of 16** end with a question. Useful for learning about the audience, but the question often becomes the final takeaway instead of the practical step.
- **8 of 16** mention a routine: BP01, BP02, BP03, BP05, BP08, BP10, BP12 and BP16. Several depend on a beginner already owning a suitable routine.
- The 16 rendered timelines run approximately **16.7–23.9 seconds**. Six beats, headline, diagram, scope label, source label and captions compete within a short interval. That is an observed layout constraint, not proof of cognitive overload or poor retention.
- Supporting posts already include the founder story, a useful run-club checklist, a lunch-planning checklist and a cross-pillar weekly plan. Those are assets to connect, not topics to recreate without a purpose.

**Highest-value improvement:** show one finished example or usable artifact in each selected episode, then make the last beat the step I can take. Put optional discussion questions in the caption or an existing story when they compete with that step.

## What could earn my attention and what could lose it

**Reasons I might stay:** the account does not shame me, sell a miracle or tell me I must buy supplements. It recognizes uncertainty about machines, soreness and missed sessions. The Blueprint identity is consistent, captions exist, and claims have visible scope and source cues.

**Reasons I might leave:** some openings sound like a general lesson rather than the situation I am in. “A short walk can have a place in your week” asks me to agree with a sensible idea; “Your calendar is full. Where does a walk fit?” gives me a problem to solve. “A better scorecard for month one” is less immediately concrete than “A month in, and the mirror looks the same?” The latter must then deliver a useful, honest process check without implying that appearance is irrelevant or promising a deadline.

**Why I might follow after watching:** I need to know the next video will help me do the next thing. A connected start-here route, recurring practical examples and a recognizable weekly rhythm can express that promise. The current serial codes and series names establish organization, but the scripts do not yet offer a clear learning path or usable next resource.

**Visual diagnosis:** the current cards mostly name categories—days, routine, setup; cost, travel, equipment; missed, next visit. They often illustrate the structure of the narration rather than completing its practical example. Keep the identity, but show the actual phone note, schedule conflict, kitchen inventory or message to an organizer. D can inhabit those situations, but a character is not required to solve the usefulness gap.

## Episode-level audit

### BP01 — First gym visit

**Keep:** a very relevant first decision; practical orientation request; acknowledgment that a video cannot check setup.

**Gap:** “bring a beginner routine” sends me to solve a large missing prerequisite. The action frame still displays category names rather than a completed first-visit note. This is an incomplete handoff, not a missing action.

**Proposed opening:** “First gym visit? Don't arrive with only a membership.”

**Visible payoff and action:** show a phone note containing my planned visit, a saved routine link and the exact orientation question: “Can someone show me how to set up the equipment in this routine?” End on “Save your plan and ask about an orientation before you go.” A real, checked start-here resource must exist before the clip points to it.

**Research boundary:** the note and question are editorial examples. Selecting/recommending an actual program needs current source, suitability and scope review. Do not disguise a generic short as individualized instruction.

### BP02 — Machines can be a valid place to start

**Keep:** the belonging line—“You do not have to earn your place at the gym with a barbell”—and the useful advice to save machine settings.

**Gap:** this contains one of the month's strongest experienced-person tips, but the diagram only shows machine versus free weight. I do not see what “save the settings” means.

**Proposed opening:** “Think machines don't count? You can start there.”

**Visible payoff and action:** show a sample note with fields for exact machine, seat/back-pad setting, load, reps and setup notes. Display a fictional illustrative entry only if numbers cannot be mistaken for a recommendation; blanks with a filled machine name are sufficient. End on “After someone checks your setup, save the settings.”

**Research boundary:** retain E06's equipment-specific skill and non-interchangeability limits. Do not imply identical displayed loads across machines or a universal safety ranking.

### BP03 — Rest between sets

**Keep:** rest is part of the session; a timer is an observable action; the current trial qualification is honest.

**Gap:** the prominent 1:00 versus 3:00 visual may be what I remember even though narration says no universal rule. The answer I need is how to apply my plan, not mainly which trial was conducted.

**Proposed opening:** “Starting the next set just because you feel awkward waiting?”

**Visible payoff and action:** a routine's rest instruction becomes a matching timer; the log records what happened in the next set. Use a placeholder or explicitly illustrative instruction unless a particular plan is actually being demonstrated. End on “Use your routine's rest guidance and record the next set.”

**Research boundary:** retain E16's small trained-men sample and limited comparison. Do not introduce a universal rest dose. This episode has a prepared citation experiment: changing its hook, scene or timing requires versioning and rebuilding both matched conditions; do not silently replace only one.

### BP04 — A short walk in a full week

**Keep:** already strong lifestyle integration: after lunch, familiar route, indoor backup. It already contains a simple cue and contingency; no mental-model lecture is needed.

**Gap:** the line and two dots are a route symbol, not a recognizable daily situation. The opening is agreeable but low in tension.

**Proposed opening:** “Your calendar is full. Where does a walk fit?”

**Visible payoff and action:** show a lunch break turning into a calendar note with a suitable route and a weather/access alternative. End on “Choose your next walking window and one backup.” D could close his laptop and head outside rather than stand under a diagram.

**Research boundary:** retain BPF01's adult scope and distinction between a starting action and full activity guidance. Do not promise a particular body outcome or universal duration. Preserve/version the existing motion experiment if changing the implementation.

### BP05 — Starting weight

**Keep:** the direct question is strong. Individual setup and a manageable learning load are sensible boundaries.

**Gap:** the hook promises a selection method; most of the answer delegates to an instructor. That can feel evasive if I cannot identify whom to ask. “Progress using your routine” also assumes I understand progression. The adult audience is briefly displaced by a spoken 16–17-year-old qualification.

**Proposed opening:** retain “What weight should you start with?”

**Visible payoff and action:** demonstrate the exact help request: “Can you check this setup and help me find a starting load for this routine?” Show where that load and the person's observation are recorded. A subsequent researched explainer should translate the routine's progression instructions; do not cram that into this clip.

**Research boundary:** a new self-selection method, load range, rep target or progression rule needs adult-specific evidence and qualified review. F02 is youth guidance and should not be the only visible authority for a primarily adult load-selection clip. State adult scope clearly; retain under-18 guidance where relevant without making the main lesson switch populations midstream.

### BP06 — Soreness

**Keep:** the existing unscheduled hook “Not sore—did the workout count?” meets the viewer at a recognizable doubt. Retain the distinction between soreness and sharp/worsening pain.

**Gap:** “keep a log” has recurred but the month has not actually taught me to make one. The current ticks suggest tracking, without demonstrating a record.

**Proposed opening:** promote the existing alternative: “Not sore—did the workout count?”

**Visible payoff and action:** show one session entry with exercise, load, reps and a note; then show the same exercise in a later session. End on “Record what you completed today.” Do not use invented improvements as if they were observed results.

**Research boundary:** preserve F08/E13 limits. Neither a completed log nor rising numbers alone proves muscle growth; no soreness is not a reason to dismiss a session.

### BP07 — First run club

**Keep:** one of the strongest specific checklists in the month; addresses a real practical mismatch hidden by “all paces.”

**Gap:** the message I would send is more useful than another list of categories. Existing BPK02 already supplies the deeper checklist, but the connection is weak.

**Proposed opening:** “The run club says ‘all paces.’ Will they actually wait for you?”

**Visible payoff and action:** show the draft: “I'm new. Which distance and pace group would suit a beginner, are walk breaks welcome, and who stays with the last group?” End with “Check those answers before you go”; link the existing checklist in the appropriate profile/caption destination.

**Research boundary:** retain editorial labeling and event-specific checks. Do not suggest all clubs are misleading or that the answers prescribe a medically suitable distance.

### BP08 — Two gym days

**Keep:** a strong constraint-led topic, concrete calendar visual and permission to plan around reality.

**Gap:** the highest-value missing answer is which routine can use those two days, not merely putting two ticks in a calendar. “Choose a beginner routine designed for the days” needs an actual resource route.

**Proposed opening:** use the existing alternative, “Only two gym days—where do you start?”

**Visible payoff and action:** show available slots becoming two calendar appointments that include travel/setup time and a link to the selected routine. Those extra minutes are scheduling considerations, not fixed prescriptions. End on “Choose a routine for your available days, then reserve the full visit.”

**Research boundary:** retain the distinction between adult public-health guidance, program design and maximum gains. Two appointments do not establish training quality or fulfill every activity requirement. Any linked program needs explicit source and scope review.

### BP09 — Protein powder

**Keep:** useful purchase-friction relief and concrete food categories; no unnecessary supplement sale.

**Gap:** “notice foods already in your meals” is a weak completion condition. It does not teach me what to do after noticing. The title is longer and less conversational than the saved alternative.

**Proposed opening:** “Do you need protein powder to start?”

**Visible payoff and action:** assemble a sample meal from familiar protein-food options, adapted to budget, preferences and dietary needs. Ask the viewer to identify a protein food in the next meal they are planning. Connect to BP11's meal-planning example instead of asking only whether marketing claims are confusing.

**Research boundary:** keep food-first flexibility and no universal intake/dose. “Enough protein,” body-composition goals or individualized amounts would be new questions requiring additional reviewed content; the visible meal must not imply complete nutritional adequacy.

### BP10 — One bad workout

**Keep:** a useful anti-overreaction principle and a specific comparison list: exercise, load, reps, rest and setup.

**Gap:** I am told to compare like with like but never see an apples-to-oranges comparison. This is an explanatory visual opportunity already supported by the script.

**Proposed opening:** “Lifted less today—should you change the whole routine?”

**Visible payoff and action:** show two fictional logs with a changed machine or rest instruction highlighted. End on “Compare the same exercise and conditions before you rewrite the plan.” The log can say “different machine” instead of inventing performance numbers.

**Research boundary:** retain editorial rather than diagnostic framing. Persistent problems deserve review. Do not turn an example into a fixed plateau threshold or recommend ignoring pain, illness or recovery concerns.

### BP11 — Tomorrow's lunch

**Keep:** likely one of the most useful lifestyle episodes on the page: a specific meal, kitchen check and short shopping list. Strong existing action; BPK03 extends it.

**Gap:** abstract ingredient circles/checklists miss the pleasurable end result. I should see lunch, not just hear that meal planning is sensible.

**Proposed opening:** “Tomorrow's lunch—and you don't want another big grocery trip?”

**Visible payoff and action:** show the food already available, the finished illustrative meal and only missing ingredients on a list. End on “Pick tomorrow's lunch, then list only what's missing.” A cooking D scene could fit naturally here.

**Research boundary:** meal and list are examples, not nutrition prescriptions. Verify food safety if introducing storage, reheating or batch-cooking instructions; the current “full week” carousel title should not imply that one meal is nutritionally complete or safely stored for a week.

### BP12 — Training to failure

**Keep:** defines failure and removes the moral framing of effort. The question directly matches common gym language.

**Gap:** an absolute beginner may not know a rep, set, effort target or RIR yet. A conceptual slider between challenging and failure does not show how a novice follows an instruction.

**Proposed opening:** “Does a set only count if you can't finish the last rep?”

**Visible payoff and action:** begin with a plain-language rep/set example or link a pre-existing glossary. Translate one actual effort instruction from a checked program. End on “Know your routine's stopping rule before the set.”

**Research boundary:** keep the uncertainty and adult population behind the research synthesis. Do not infer a universal RIR target or suggest beginners estimate proximity to failure precisely. Demonstrating an exertional failure attempt requires more care than an abstract illustration.

### BP13 — Month-one scorecard

**Keep:** process review avoids a false transformation deadline. Visits, setup skills and recording habits are controllable observations.

**Gap:** a man who came for aesthetics may hear “look at attendance instead” as avoiding his original goal. Acknowledge the desire and distinguish progress signals from the eventual outcome.

**Proposed opening:** “A month in, and the mirror looks the same?”

**Visible payoff and action:** show a three-line check: “Did I attend? Can I set up? Did I record?” Then circle one practical adjustment for the coming week. End on “Choose one thing to improve next week.”

**Research boundary:** retain that process metrics are not a body-composition test, and make no appearance deadline. A new timeline, body-measurement method or bulk/cut recommendation needs separate evidence and context.

### BP14 — Sleep schedule

**Keep:** the current hook is already concise and relevant. It recognizes shift work and caring responsibilities rather than assuming unlimited control.

**Gap:** “pick one change” gives a tired person another design task. Illustrative clocks do not identify what is crowding sleep out.

**Proposed opening:** retain “Your workouts have a schedule. Does your sleep?”

**Visible payoff and action:** show an ordinary evening calendar, identify one movable task and place a realistic wind-down reminder. Offer this as one option, not the solution for everyone. End on “Choose one feasible schedule change for this week.”

**Research boundary:** retain BPF03/CDC scope and professional help for ongoing problems. A reminder is an editorial planning tool, not a treatment for insomnia; do not invent a universal bedtime, sleep dose or shift-work cure.

### BP15 — Try an activity

**Keep:** expands the brand beyond lifting and allows curiosity without an identity commitment; the closing question is one of the month's most expressive.

**Gap:** “pick something” and a generic access checklist are broad. The content could be more memorable by showing one ordinary decision while retaining options.

**Proposed opening:** “You can try a class without making it your whole personality.”

**Visible payoff and action:** compare a fictional beginner session's travel, cost, available instruction and equipment with the viewer's real week. End on “Check one beginner session's practical details.” The existing “What would you try…?” question can stay in the caption or follow-up story.

**Research boundary:** editorial wording and fictional events must be labeled. Do not imply one activity meets every goal, guarantee habit formation, or recommend a paid venue without current verification.

### BP16 — Missed workout

**Keep:** psychologically kind, practical, and already contains a cause/adjustment loop. Avoiding automatic punishment volume is a useful boundary.

**Gap:** “one barrier, one adjustment” is shown as an X and a tick; the actual adjustment is missing. This is the clearest opportunity for a short lived scenario.

**Proposed opening:** “Work ran late. You missed the gym. Now what?”

**Visible payoff and action:** D receives an overtime message; the example calendar checks the routine's missed-day guidance and chooses a realistic next visit. A preparation barrier might become a bag by the door; a schedule barrier might become a different slot. End on “Choose the next session and change one thing that blocked this one.”

**Research boundary:** label this a fictional planning example, not individualized programming or a guaranteed adherence method. Do not prescribe replacement volume, reduced volume or recovery timing without checking the actual routine and context.

## What is genuinely missing from this month

These are candidate audience jobs, not evidence that every one deserves a November slot.

| Missing job | Why it matters to this persona | Smallest useful deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Where do I get a suitable first routine?” | Half the month mentions a routine, and the initial action assumes one | A checked start-here path with a small number of clearly scoped resources; no invented universal program |
| “What do sets, reps and the notation in my plan mean?” | Later videos assume language I may not understand | One visual translation of an actual plan instruction; glossary connected to the relevant clips |
| “What exactly do I record?” | BP02/05/06/10/13 depend on recording and comparison | A copyable simple log with a worked fictional entry and a note that machine settings/loads are equipment-specific |
| “What do I do when the machine is occupied?” | A normal first-visit friction absent from the month | A polite ask/wait/check-approved-alternative example, without inventing interchangeable exercises |
| “How do I know what to change next time?” | Progression is named but not taught | Decode one checked routine's own progression rule; distinguish its rule from universal advice |
| “What do I pack and how do I fit the trip in?” | Scheduling two sessions is insufficient if travel/setup is ignored | An ordinary preparation checklist and a realistic calendar block; tailor to the gym, access and transport |
| “How does getting fitter relate to the body I want?” | The brand promises aesthetics, health and capability, but mostly covers logistics/myths | A scoped expectations explainer that acknowledges appearance and separates training, nutrition, recovery, time and individual variation; fresh evidence needed for outcome claims |
| “How do meals, eating out and social plans fit?” | Nutrition appears mainly as optional powder and one lunch | A practical meal-choice or social-plan scenario; avoid moral food rules and unreviewed calorie targets |
| “What should I do today?” | The calendar is a publishing plan, not a complete beginner course | A short start-here route and one clearly named action/resource per relevant post |

“How to lose fat,” “how much protein,” and “should I bulk or cut” are plausible future research questions, not automatic additions. They need demand validation, population/context boundaries and stronger care around appearance-focused advice. Do not displace approachable lifestyle content merely because classic gym topics are familiar.

## A clearer learning route without expanding the month

Keep all posts independently understandable because viewers will arrive out of order. Add a visible optional route at the profile/resource level:

1. **Get ready:** account promise → choose a suitable routine/resource → BP01 first visit → BP08 fit the week.
2. **Do the session:** plain-language notation/log → BP02 setup record → BP05 load/setup help → BP03 rest → BP12 effort.
3. **Make the week easier:** BP04 walking → BP09 food options → BP11 lunch → BP14 recovery; BP07 and BP15 for accessible activities and enjoyment.
4. **Keep going:** BP06 and BP10 interpret sessions → BP16 return after disruption → BP13 review progress.

This is a proposed navigation path, not a recommendation to renumber or reschedule the current production silently. A pinned orientation video can name the audience and promise, but it does not by itself fill the missing practical resource path.

## Practical prioritization

1. **Resolve the routine/log prerequisite.** A polished hook that still sends me to an undefined routine will not fix the problem.
2. **Prototype three varied episodes, not the entire month:** BP01 (action clarity), BP11 (visible meal payoff), BP16 (recognizable conflict and adjustment). BP02 is a low-cost fourth if capacity allows.
3. **Preserve the useful boundaries while simplifying presentation.** Keep the claim-specific source cue and full references. Do not remove qualifications that change the meaning of the advice. Reduce repeated category labels and redundant wording before reducing scientific nuance.
4. **Use D where a person experiencing the problem helps.** A meal, bag, work message or calendar scene gives him a role. A corner mascot alone does not repair a missing example.
5. **Do not add a spoken mental-model name.** Show the practical decision. The user should not need to learn “implementation intentions” to choose a walking time and backup.
6. **Make the final action copyable or demonstrable.** If discussion is the purpose of an episode, ask one clear question; otherwise the companion story/caption can collect responses without replacing the payoff.

## Validation before a broad rework

Use the project's existing adult-beginner research protocol where possible rather than creating a second competing sample plan. These additions are task prompts and observation fields, not a claim that the test has run.

- After the opening: “What do you expect this video to help you do?” Check whether the answer matches the delivered lesson.
- After the clip, without replay: “What would you do next?” Ask for the actual note, message or calendar choice when applicable, not just “I would follow the advice.”
- “What information would you still need before doing that?” Specifically code missing routine, terminology, equipment setup, scheduling and resource access.
- “What do you expect this account to help with next?” Distinguish a clear repeatable promise from general aesthetic liking.
- Record the existing comprehension question and critical misunderstanding for the episode. A more exciting version must still preserve the important boundary.
- Compare the graphics baseline with a defined revised version. If hook, visual example, ending and D all change, call it a **creative-package comparison**, not proof that the character caused an effect.
- Separate stated follow interest from observed completion of a small task and from eventual platform follows/retention. None substitutes automatically for the others.
- Log hands-on drafting, visual preparation, corrections and review. A successful concept still has to fit the owner's 12-hour weekly production ceiling.

Do not infer retention improvement from this desk review. The intended outcome is a testable improvement: a beginner can identify the relevance sooner, understand the answer, complete one bounded action and know what useful help comes next.
