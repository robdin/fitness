# Monetization review · Blueprint V6

27 October 2026. Independent specialist audit of the current local project. Recommendations only: no commercial offer, recruitment, collection of contacts, checkout or public resource was launched.

**Verdict:** proceed with the bounded audience-and-production pilot once its existing launch checks pass. Do not require revenue in month one. The most useful commercial improvement is to learn whether a beginner uses a small practical resource after watching—not to build a paid fitness product now. Audience interest and a sustainable four-video week within twelve hands-on hours remain the actual success criteria. No customer, payment, recurring use or measured production-cost evidence exists yet.

## What is already strong

- BC01, BC08 and BC16 form a coherent problem: getting started, making room in a real week and returning when the plan is interrupted. This is a more concrete potential source of value than selling generic fitness information.
- The existing starter kit supplies copyable gym questions, scheduling prompts, a training-log example and a lunch list. It also clearly distinguishes planning help from individualized programming.
- BC09 explicitly says a beginner need not buy protein powder to start. That supports a helpful, low-pressure commercial posture. Preserve it even if sponsorship opportunities arrive later.
- The framework already separates a useful follower from a paying customer, first use from repeat use, and revenue from owner income. The commerce code explicitly does not claim a checkout is a payment or that event import fulfills a product.

## The smallest path from content to useful behavior

Use the existing **My Real Week** section as a one-screen “Real Week Card,” with its worked example accessible immediately below it. Keep the rest of the kit available as optional sections; do not make every viewer complete the entire kit. This is a formatting/navigation proposal, not a new product requirement.

Suggested card:

```text
MY NEXT SESSION
Day / time / place:
Whole trip, including travel and changing:
The routine or activity I am following:
If that slot falls through, my next step:
```

Suggested CTA **only after the destination exists and works**:

> “Want the blank version? Open ‘My Real Week’ from our profile and copy the four lines into your notes.”

For YouTube Shorts, do not rely on an external URL in the description or comments: those URLs are non-clickable; channel profile links are clickable. Test the actual viewer route on a phone. A missing or awkward destination is a distribution failure, not proof nobody wants the resource. [YouTube: Sharing links with your audiences](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639?hl=en)

Keep the video’s useful action intact. Put this optional route in profile/navigation or appropriate supporting copy; do not replace every closing action with a download pitch. Log any addition with date and version so later observations reflect the actual experience. No email gate, automated DM keyword or multi-step funnel is needed for this test.

## Learn about value without turning month one into a sales launch

Use a small, explicitly nonrepresentative subset of the existing target-viewer research when participation is authorized. Six to ten resource sessions are an operating choice, not a statistical demand estimate. Allow at most about an hour of incremental investigation per week **inside** the twelve-hour budget; reduce optional work to make room or omit the commercial module.

1. **Before showing the resource:** “Tell me about the last time a workout or activity did not fit your day. What did you do?” Then ask where they looked, what they used and what remained unresolved. Include people for whom this is not a meaningful problem.
2. **Observe one task:** “Use whichever section fits that situation, if any. You can also decide this is not useful.” Avoid filling the card for them. Record completed/not completed/declined and why. A completed calendar entry is a planning artifact, not proof of adherence or health improvement.
3. **Ask about substitution:** “What, if anything, would this replace?” and “Have you paid for help with this problem before, and did you use it?” Prior purchases provide context, not proof they will buy from this brand.
4. **After seven to fourteen days, only with an agreed follow-up:** ask whether another occasion arose, whether the tool was used, what changed and what still required help. No later opportunity is different from refusing to use it; missing follow-up remains unknown.
5. **Separate the evidence:** a click is access intent; copying is initial use; an unaided useful artifact is activation; another real occasion can establish repeat use. Likes, saves and “I would buy this” are not payments.

Keep pseudonymous IDs and aggregate outcomes in the repository; keep consent/contact mappings and identifying free text private. Do not ask for physique photos, body measurements or medical histories for this planning test. Reuse existing response records where possible rather than creating a second dashboard.

**Proposed decision for investigating a paid offer, not a new content-pilot gate:** after the content pilot, continue to offer discovery only if several independent participants describe the same recent friction, at least three of six observed attempts produce a useful artifact without the operator doing the task, and at least two people later use it again or describe a concrete remaining need. These are deliberately provisional operating hurdles, not validated thresholds or market prevalence. A successful free tool with no remaining paid problem is a good audience resource, not a reason to invent a paid product. Repeated non-use, a trivial workaround or a need outside the team’s competence means keep it free, narrow it or stop commercial work.

## Optional offer hypothesis for later

The strongest hypothesis to investigate is **help applying a routine someone already has to the week they actually live**, not another generic workout PDF. This is an inference from the current content and resource design, not evidence of willingness to pay. Stronger by Science currently advertises six full programs plus a builder as a free download, illustrating a substantial free substitute for generic programming. This does not prove that no one buys programs or that its offering is appropriate for every viewer. [Stronger by Science program bundle](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/program-bundle/)

Example research concept, not a live offer:

> **Real-Week Setup Session** — a one-time, 45-minute small-group planning walkthrough. Bring a routine you already understand. Use a blank calendar card and two worked examples to identify travel time, realistic slots and questions you need to resolve with your instructor. Includes the editable card and a short recap. It does not design or change your workout, set nutrition targets or review injuries. A possible research price is $29 once; nothing is for sale yet.

Ask what it would replace, what is missing and why they would decline before asking whether the price seems reasonable. A live session may prove unnecessary or consume too much owner time; do not build it from this concept alone. If a later paid pilot is justified, its actual deliverables, dates, audience scope, support limits, total price, access and refund process must be concrete before payment. This is a separate work decision, not implicit authorization from this audit.

Start with at most one offer and one clear outcome. Do not simultaneously launch a membership, coaching service and template shop. If the real unmet need is choosing or changing exercise programming, recognize that this requires a different service and competent review; do not stretch the planning offer to cover it.

## Economics: illustrative scenarios, not forecasts

No demand, conversion rate, platform payout or actual provider charge is assumed. The owner has specified time capacity but not a recurring cash ceiling, labor valuation or required income. Select a cash ceiling before authorizing new paid production; do not turn the examples below into spending permission.

For a **four-week, sixteen-video** cycle at the full twelve-hour ceiling:

| Assumed scenario | Hands-on hours | Assumed value/hour | Assumed cash costs | Economic cost for cycle | Economic cost/core video |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Lower valuation example | 48 | $25 | $100 | $1,300 | $81.25 |
| Higher valuation example | 48 | $50 | $300 | $2,700 | $168.75 |

These assume the forty-eight hours include supporting content, review, publishing, community and measurement. Cash cost should include incremental generation, failed attempts, relevant tool allocation, review services, storage and distribution. Keep sunk setup work and one-time rebuilds visible separately. Do not add the same annual subscription allocation and its full cash payment twice. Cost per core video here allocates the whole cycle across sixteen episodes; it is not a standalone rendering cost.

For a hypothetical one-time digital offer, assume a **6% blended fees/refunds reserve** and **$1 variable cash cost per sale**. These are invented planning inputs, not any payment provider’s fee schedule. Also assume five extra support minutes per order valued at $50/hour:

| Assumed one-time price | Contribution before owner support | Contribution after assumed support | Orders to cover the $2,700 example | Additional support hours at that order count |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $19 | $16.86 | $12.69 | 213 | 17.75 |
| $29 | $26.26 | $22.09 | 123 | 10.25 |

Formula: `price × (1 − reserve) − variable cash − support hours × hourly value`. Breakeven orders are rounded up using the unrounded contribution. Acquisition costs, taxes and product development are excluded, so this is incomplete economics—not earnings guidance. The support hours are **additional** to the forty-eight hours; they violate the existing capacity if no other work is reduced. Increasing revenue can make the process less sustainable.

For the optional group concept, ten hypothetical attendees at $29 produce $290 gross. With the same 6% reserve, $1 per attendee and four total fulfillment hours valued at $50/hour, only **$62.60** remains before shared content costs, acquisition, taxes or product development. Four hours is an assumption including preparation, session and follow-up—not just the forty-five-minute event. This illustrates why a small paid pilot can test delivery without proving a viable income stream.

The existing `fitness_os/economics.py` models membership arithmetic. It should not label these one-time sales MRR. `fitness_os/commerce.py` imports signed event observations; it is not a live checkout, customer-access system, refund workflow or sales-validation result. No additional billing engineering is necessary to run the current audience pilot.

## Before, during and after

**Before:** reconcile the historical business-plan pointers; ensure no promised resource link leads to a local file or nonexistent destination; keep unknown economics explicit. Finish existing human/audio/platform readiness checks. Do not hold video testing for a monetization build.

**During:** measure actual creation labor and charges; observe one optional resource task with consenting viewers; ask about recent problems and existing alternatives; preserve the frozen content-pilot thresholds. Reconcile commercial-research minutes within the twelve-hour cap.

**After:** only if useful behavior and a coherent remaining problem recur, scope one concrete offer and its fulfillment economics. Treat any subsequent paid pilot as a separate experiment. Require actual paid delivery and use before claiming willingness to pay; require repeated need and actual renewal before claiming recurring revenue.

**Do not build yet:** a custom app, login portal, subscription, paid community, personalized AI coach, transformation challenge, large course, automated sales DMs, affiliate storefront or advertising funnel. Do not buy traffic to manufacture a commercial signal. Platform monetization eligibility and payout rates are not relevant launch gates at this stage and were not estimated.

## Trust constraints

Keep evidence selection independent of prospective sponsors. Preserve the “you may not need to buy anything” answer when it is the useful answer. Identify any future paid relationship clearly and keep a no-purchase alternative where it serves the viewer. Do not let a source citation imply the worksheet or commercial service has been clinically validated. Do not sell an aspirational body image as a promised outcome. The broad brand mission can remain ambitious while each actual deliverable stays narrow and honest.
