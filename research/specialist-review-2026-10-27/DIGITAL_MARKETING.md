## Positioning: keep the ambition, make the entry point concrete

Keep the mission across strength, movement, nutrition, recovery and the life around them. Do not rename the account around lifting or narrow it to an anime audience. The initial situation can be narrower than the long-term mission:

> **Science-backed fitness for beginners with a full calendar.**

Supporting explanation:

> Practical help with training, food and recovery—one useful next step that fits your week.

Keep **“Your dream body. Your real life.”** as the emotional brand line. Use the beginner/full-calendar sentence to make the category clear. This is a proposed expression of the existing positioning, not a validated winner or an additional brand direction.

For recruitment and interpretation, look first at adults roughly 18–35 who are starting or restarting and can describe a recent scheduling, confidence or information problem. Include the requested new-male-beginner perspective as one useful subgroup while preserving the current inclusive audience. Do not infer demographic preferences from the character's appearance or generalize adult guidance to teenagers.

The possible distinction is not merely “science-backed”: it is **showing what a useful fitness decision looks like in an ordinary week**. The proof is already present in V6: a message to a gym, saved machine settings, a whole-trip calendar entry, a lunch inventory and a missed-session decision. Keep showing those artifacts. Do not promise that the branded approach itself is clinically validated, or that 20% of effort delivers 80% of results.

The owner need not settle every future product or commission a new logo before launch. A selected working public name, consistent descriptor, existing Blueprint styling and matching account identities are sufficient for this pilot. Name/handle availability is not verified by this audit.

## Build the smallest complete first-visit path

Use the existing assets rather than commissioning another month:

1. **Orientation:** BC00 explains who the account serves and why to return.
2. **First practical win:** BC01 shows what to ask before a first gym visit.
3. **Make it usable:** the starter kit supplies the copyable message and one blank note/log.
4. **Continue:** published episodes about making the whole trip fit, keeping a useful record and returning after disruption.

The current kit route is BC00 → BC01 → BC08 → BC06/BC10 → BC16. That is a proposed learning route, not the publication order. On November 1, BC08 and BC16 will not yet be published under the proposed schedule. Do not create broken links or suggest those videos are already available. Populate navigation progressively, linking only to verified published destinations. The kit can supply its existing scheduling and restart notes immediately without releasing later cohort videos early.

**Instagram:** propose BC00 as the first pinned item, BC01 as the practical entry item, and the existing founder carousel BCK01 once published. Later, the third position can feature an especially useful published resource after a documented review. This uses the three-post grid-pinning feature documented by Meta; it is an orientation choice, not a promise of reach. Check the current account UI before configuring it. [Meta's pinning announcement](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2022/06/grid_pinning_on_profile/)

**YouTube:** use a channel trailer/featured section and a short “Start here” playlist, rather than describing it as an Instagram-style pin. YouTube documents separate trailer, returning-subscriber feature and playlist sections on the channel Home tab. Select BC00 where the actual channel UI permits it; otherwise put it first in the visible starter playlist. [YouTube channel layout](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3219384?hl=en)

A Short can link to a related video from the same channel, including another Short, with advanced feature access. The destination must be public or unlisted. Keep **private experiment controls out of this route** and link only to the intended public cohort/orientation asset. Use BC01 → BC00 initially if that helps orientation; add other relevant destinations only after release. Adding a link to an unlisted future cohort episode would expose it early and undermine the planned release window. [YouTube related-video requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)

For the starter kit, the minimum useful public destination is a mobile-readable page with the copyable tools, sources, and links to available episodes. Do not send beginners to a production review hub, source ZIP, local `file://` URL or an unexplained repository tree. A public kit is helpful but need not block a video-only pilot if no public CTA promises it. Where it is offered, test it signed out on a phone. No email gate is needed to learn whether people find it useful.

**YouTube search:** explicit topic language can improve clarity without rewriting the spoken hook. YouTube says Shorts search considers metadata matching and whether people click and watch. That supports honest descriptive titles; it does not establish a keyword recipe or guaranteed ranking. Tags have little discovery importance beyond cases such as misspellings. ## Tests worth running within the existing pilot

### 1. Does the profile make the account useful to navigate?

Add a brief navigation block **after** the existing comprehension questions, so it cannot prime their answers. Show the actual profile and ask: “Who is this for?”, “What would you expect next?”, and “Find something you could use before your next visit.” Record completion, wrong expectations and broken destinations. Because they have already seen a clip, call this a post-exposure navigation check, not a cold-profile conversion experiment. A genuinely cold profile comparison would need fresh first-exposure participants and can wait.

Use repeated expectation of a complete workout prescription as a reason to clarify the promise or supply a properly reviewed path to one. Do not solve it by publishing a hurried routine. An intro wording alternative, if needed, is **“We make fitness research useful: clearer training decisions, simple food ideas, and ways to fit them into your week.”** This preserves scope and avoids implying that every planning tip is itself a research finding.

### 2. Do viewers find the next useful thing?

Retain the protocol's primary gates unchanged. Add lightweight diagnostic observations where actually available: subscribers/follows attributed to an episode, profile activity, views originating from a starter playlist, and aggregate visits to a working resource. Record the platform's field name and observation window, plus missingness. A click is interest, not proof of a completed behavior change; a follow is not evidence of willingness to pay.

YouTube's current help distinguishes engaged views from play starts, and calculates Shorts APV/average view duration among those who stayed. Keep the existing definitions and avoid using generic view counts as the denominator for a different claim. [YouTube content-performance definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)

Do not create a misleading person-level funnel by dividing unrelated aggregates such as all weekly follows by all profile visits. Attribution and time windows may not line up. Report counts and limited, explicitly defined rates only where the actual data supports them. This audit adds no follower, click or conversion threshold to the go/no-go decision.

### 3. Which everyday problem earns a reason to return?

At the fixed readout, combine episode-level attention, specific useful responses and the questions people ask next. Treat the six/two/one strength/food/recovery coverage imbalance as a limitation. A food video doing well does not establish a general food-content winner; a single sleep post cannot falsify the recovery pillar. Choose one concrete next-month question from recurring audience evidence, then test it in the next bounded batch.

Keep Trial Reels, frequent metadata edits, character-wide rollouts and additional channels out of the current frozen cohort. Meta's Trial Reels feature changes who sees content first and can automatically release a successful trial to followers; that is a different distribution design, not a randomized A/B test. It can be evaluated separately later if the account has access. [Meta's Trial Reels description](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/)
